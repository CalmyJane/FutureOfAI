# Third review: Chapter 9 — Information, Media and Democracy

## Verdict
Ready after the MUST fixes below. The chapter is unusually careful: every study number, quotation and legal provision I checked against its source matched (about 250 claims across two rounds), the persuasion and detection sections reflect their papers accurately, including the Costello correction history, and statements about named politicians and companies stay within their sources. The blocking problems are about currency, not accuracy. The US midterm paragraph still reads as if the vote of 3 November 2026 were to come and omits the most serious documented AI incident of that campaign (a Russia-linked deepfake against the very candidate the paragraph names). The UK section says the UK "has gone further in one respect" when since 29 June 2026 it also criminalises making or supplying nudification tools, which matters for the Grok case. Post-election reporting supports, and does not change, the chapter's conclusion that AI use grew without demonstrated effects on results.

### M2. UK law on nudification tools omitted; "in one respect" now wrong
- Where: textbook/chapters/ch09.md:241, :246 (Grok box, last two sentences) and Table 9.3 (:249–258)
- Text now (241): "The United Kingdom has gone further in one respect. Since 6 February 2026 it has been a criminal offence in England and Wales to create an intimate deepfake of an adult ..."
- Text now (246): "The AI Act's new prohibition of non-consensual intimate image generation, applicable from 2 December 2026, will also be enforced after the fact, but it binds the provider of the generating system itself ..."
- Problem: OUTDATED / incomplete as of the book's own "as of October 2026" cut-off. Section 99 of the Crime and Policing Act 2026 inserted s. 66I into the Sexual Offences Act 2003: an offence to make, adapt, supply or offer to supply "a thing ... for use as a generator of purported intimate images" (up to 3 years on indictment; defence of having taken all reasonable steps to prevent non-consensual use). It has been in force since 29 June 2026 (S.I. 2026/689). The UK therefore goes further in two respects, and the Grok box's point that only the AI Act binds the maker of the generating system is no longer true for the UK. A UK lawyer would notice the omission in a table titled "Main legal instruments governing platforms and synthetic media, as of October 2026".
- Evidence: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/20/section/99 — "66I Making or supplying purported intimate image generators (1) A person commits an offence if the person— (a) makes or adapts a thing, or (b) supplies or offers to supply a thing, for use as a generator of purported intimate images." Version history shows the 29/06/2026 amendment; commencement on 29 June 2026 also reported by Home Office circular 004/2026 (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/circular-0042026-crime-and-policing-act-2026/circular-0042026-crime-and-policing-act-2026-accessible) and Lawtons/PHB commentary.
- Change to:
  - Line 241: "The United Kingdom has gone further in two respects. Since 6 February 2026 it has been a criminal offence in England and Wales to create an intimate deepfake of an adult, such as a nude or sexual image, without consent, or to request that one be made, even if the image is never shared. These are summary offences, punishable by a fine or a short prison sentence [@ukparliament2025s138]. Since 29 June 2026 it has also been an offence to make, adapt or supply a tool for generating such images, unless the supplier took all reasonable steps to prevent its use without consent [@ukparliament2026s99]."
  - Line 246, replace "but it binds the provider of the generating system itself, here the company ..." with "but it binds the provider of the generating system itself, as the UK's offence of supplying intimate-image generators now also does, here the company ...".
  - Table 9.3, add a row after the s. 138 row: "| UK Crime and Policing Act 2026, s. 99 (new s. 66I Sexual Offences Act 2003), from 29 Jun 2026 (England and Wales) | Makers and suppliers of generator tools | Offence of making, adapting or supplying a generator of purported intimate images | Criminal courts; up to 3 years' imprisonment |" and add @ukparliament2026s99 to the table source.
