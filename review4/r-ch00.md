# Review 4: Preface (textbook/chapters/ch00-preface.md)

Reviewer scope: the preface as a reader would meet it, its factual claims, and the AI-authorship section "How the book was made". I compared that section with the copyright-page note (`textbook/print/build.py`, `AI_NOTE` and `front_matter`). The back cover and the rest of the production checks are in `r-production.md`.

## Verdict

Ready after fixes. The preface is clear, well organised and unusually candid. The disclosure says plainly that most of the text was generated by AI, names the models, and says that no independent human expert has reviewed the whole book. It also admits that the review rounds kept finding misquotations. There are two problems:

1. One factual statement about the process will be false once this round is finished.
2. One description of the checking is wider than what was done.

Both are easy to fix.

## MUST change before printing

### M1. Number of review rounds and the "last round" figures are out of date
- Where: textbook/chapters/ch00-preface.md:52
- Text now: "Three such review rounds were run. In the last, about 2,600 statements were checked against their sources; the errors and out-of-date statements it found were corrected before printing."
- Problem: OUTDATED / INCONSISTENT. This review is a fourth round. The repository has `review/`, `review2/`, `review3/` and now `review4/`, and `review4/BRIEF.md` calls it "the last check before the author prints it". After this round the sentence understates the process. Its claim about "the last" round (2,600 statements) also describes round 3, not the round that actually came last. The clause "were corrected before printing" is only true if every MUST item from round 4 is applied. I could not verify the 2,600 figure, because the logs are in `textbook/work/` and the brief says not to read them.
- Evidence: `review4/BRIEF.md`, line 3: "Your review is the last check before the author prints it"; the directory listing shows four review folders.
- Change to: "Four such review rounds were run. In the last, about [N] statements were checked against their sources; the errors and out-of-date statements found were corrected before printing." Take N from the round-4 tallies (`review4/00-VERDICT.md`). If some round-4 items are deliberately left unfixed, write "most of the errors" instead of "the errors".

### M2. "Checked its statements against the cited sources" claims more than was done
- Where: textbook/chapters/ch00-preface.md:52
- Text now: "separate instances, which had not seen the earlier work, read each chapter as a reader would, checked its statements against the cited sources, and recomputed its worked examples."
- Problem: UNSUPPORTED (overclaim about checking). A reader will take this to mean that every statement was compared with its cited source. In practice the AI reviewers could only open sources that are freely available online. In this round, for example, I could not open the full text of Haigh's *Communications of the ACM* column on the publisher's site (HTTP 403; I found a copy elsewhere). I also could not open Buchanan and Shortliffe's 1984 MYCIN book, the 1958 *New York Times* article (cited only "as reproduced in" a 2024 book), or paywalled journal articles. Those statements were checked against secondary copies or abstracts, or not checked at all, and each report lists them under "Could not verify". The next sentence of the preface ("about 2,600 statements were checked") also implies a sample rather than every statement. The sentence should say that.
- Evidence: this review's "Could not verify" sections (r-ch00, r-ch01, r-ch02); for example, `https://cacm.acm.org/opinion/how-the-ai-boom-went-bust/` returned HTTP 403 on 26 November 2026.
- Change to: "separate instances, which had not seen the earlier work, read each chapter as a reader would, checked a large sample of its statements against the cited sources where those sources could be read online (books and paywalled articles often could not), and recomputed its worked examples."

## SHOULD change

### S1. Who "the author" is in "calculation by the author" and "in the author's view"
- Where: textbook/chapters/ch00-preface.md:44 (and the many captions and sentences that use this phrase in Chapters 1–14, e.g. ch01.md:268, ch01.md:276, ch02.md:33, ch02.md:259)
- Text now: "Numbers marked as computed by the author (for example the tokenisation and probability tables in Chapter 3) were produced with publicly available models and software, and the settings are given. Worked examples, calculations and tables that name no source are the author's own"
- Problem: UNCLEAR. The disclosure says the AI agents drafted the text. The calculations and the "in the author's view" judgements were therefore generated by the models and approved by the human author. They were not worked out by the human author. A reader who reads Chapter 2 without the preface will assume that "Calculation by the author" means the person on the title page. One sentence fixes this.
- Evidence: ch00-preface.md:52–54 ("The chapters were researched, drafted and revised by the large language models … The author did not write most of the sentences in it").
- Change to: add at the end of line 44: "Throughout, "the author" refers to the book as directed and approved by Calmy Jane; calculations so labelled were carried out by the AI agents described below and recomputed in review, and opinions so labelled are positions the author has adopted."

### S2. Check that the list of models is complete and in the right order
- Where: textbook/chapters/ch00-preface.md:52; same wording in build.py `AI_NOTE` (copyright page)
- Text now: "The chapters were researched, drafted and revised by the large language models Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.2"
- Problem: UNCLEAR / possibly incomplete. Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 on 22 September 2026 and Claude Fable 5.2 on 9 November 2026. The preface says evidence was gathered from October onwards, and the copyright page says web sources were checked from 20 October. So any drafting done between 20 October and 9 November must have used some other model, perhaps Fable 5.1. If so, the disclosure leaves out a model that wrote part of the text. I could not check this without the work logs, which the brief puts out of bounds. The author should confirm the list.
- Evidence: TechCrunch, 22 Sep 2026, https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/22/anthropic-releases-opus-5-5-with-lower-prices-and-fable-level-performance/ ("Anthropic releases Opus 5.5…"); 9to5Mac, 9 Nov 2026, https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/09/anthropic-upgrades-claude-with-new-fable-5-2-model-details-here/ ("Anthropic upgrades Claude with new Fable 5.2 model").
- Change to (if earlier models were used): "…by the large language models Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Fable 5.1 and, from its release on 9 November 2026, Claude Fable 5.2, made by Anthropic…" Make the same change in `AI_NOTE`.

### S3. Say what "approved the text" means in practice
- Where: textbook/chapters/ch00-preface.md:54
- Text now: "The author chose the subject and the scope, set the rules for how evidence is used and labelled (described above), made the editorial decisions, and approved the text."
- Problem: UNCLEAR. "Approved the text" could mean that the author read all 626 pages, or that the author signed off on the process. The disclosure is honest elsewhere. Saying which one applies would complete it, because it tells readers how much human judgement stands behind each sentence. (The copyright page uses the same wording: "directed and approved the work".)
- Change to: state whichever is true, e.g. "…made the editorial decisions, and read and approved the final text" or "…made the editorial decisions and approved the text for publication without reading every page".

## Could not verify
- "about 2,600 statements were checked against their sources" (line 52). This figure comes from round-3 logs in `textbook/work/`, which are out of bounds.
- That the reviewing instances "had not seen the earlier work". This is plausible for round 4, whose brief forbids reading earlier reviews, but I cannot check it for rounds 1–3.
- Whether models other than Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.2 took part (see S2).

## Checked and confirmed
- "more than 1,200 references": the printed reference list runs to 1,293 numbered entries (PDF pp. 529 onwards). The bib files hold 1,295 unique keys.
- The dates in "gathered and checked between October and late November 2026" match the copyright page ("Web sources were checked between 20 October and 26 November 2026").
- The copyright-page AI note (build.py `AI_NOTE`) is consistent with the preface: "Most of the text of this book was generated by AI … fact-checked and reviewed by the same models … See 'How the book was made' in the preface."
- The chapter outline (Parts I–V, Chapters 1–14) matches the chapter titles in the PDF table of contents (Part I: Chapters 1–4, etc.).
- Both named models exist and are Anthropic models: Claude Opus 5.5 (released 22 Sep 2026) and Claude Fable 5.2 (released 9 Nov 2026).
- The statement "no independent human expert has reviewed the whole book" is accurate as far as the repository shows. It is clear and should stay.
- The evidence-labelling rules (lines 33–42) are applied in Chapters 1 and 2 as far as I sampled: preprints, company reports, company usage data, leaderboards and commentary are labelled.
