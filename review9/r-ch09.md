## SHOULD change

### S1. Pew comparison of models: "best-performing" overstates what Pew tested (balance between companies)
- Where: textbook/chapters/ch09.md:179
- Text now: "The best-performing of the models Pew tested, which included systems from OpenAI and Anthropic, was Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.6. It still missed the human results by 12.4 percentage points on average across the three waves."
- Problem: UNCLEAR / balance. Pew ran its main study on Claude Opus 4.6. It compared that model with only one other, OpenAI's GPT-5.1, and only on a subset of questions. Opus did "slightly better" there (11.4 against 13.3 points). Pew says both would mislead, in opposite directions: Opus towards middle-of-the-road answers, GPT towards extremes. In a book written mainly by Anthropic models, "the best-performing of the models Pew tested" reads as a wider ranking than Pew gave.
- Evidence: Pew, "How synthetic polling results change based on the AI model", 30 Sep 2026 (https://www.pewresearch.org/data-labs/2026/09/30/how-synthetic-polling-results-change-based-on-the-ai-model/): "Opus performed slightly better, with an average absolute error of 11.4 percentage points" (GPT-5.1: 13.3). Pew, "How well synthetic samples replicate public opinion": "We tested multiple models, but all synthetic results unless noted otherwise come from Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.6"; table "Average across waves … Total 12.4".
- Change to: "Pew ran its main tests with Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.6. In a comparison on a subset of questions, Opus came slightly closer to the human results than OpenAI's GPT-5.1 (an average error of 11.4 against 13.3 points), and Pew said that either model would mislead. Across the three waves, Opus missed the human results by 12.4 percentage points on average."
