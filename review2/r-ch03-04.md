# Second review: Chapters 3 (How LLMs work) and 4 (Scaling)

Reviewer scope: `textbook/chapters/ch03.md` (358 lines, ~10,900 words) and `textbook/chapters/ch04.md` (250 lines, ~9,300 words), bib/ch03.bib, bib/ch04.bib, Figures 3.1–3.3 and 4.1–4.3, Tables 3.1–3.8 and 4.1–4.3, and the printed pages 63–135 of `delivery/understanding-ai_A5_interior.pdf`. I read every line of both chapters. I re-ran the author's computations myself: tiktoken 0.14.0, the Sennrich BPE code, NumPy, and Hugging Face transformers 5.20.2 with PyTorch 2.14.1 on GPT-2 small and Qwen2.5-1.5B. I pulled Epoch's `notable_ai_models.csv` and checked primary pages and abstracts on the web.

## 1. Verdict: **publish after minor fixes**

Five things decide it:

1. **The technical content is accurate.** I checked about 70 discrete claims and found two small factual errors, both outside the core explanation. Every number the author says he computed that I could recompute came out exactly as printed: the tokenisations, the BPE merges, the attention example, the GPT-2 parameter count, the KV-cache sizes, the next-token probabilities, the nucleus sizes and all the scaling-law arithmetic. This is rare and is the chapters' main strength.
2. **The 2025–2026 material holds up against the primary data.** That covers the Epoch database (GPT-6 Astra 9.4 × 10^26 FLOP "Likely", GPT-4.5 187M H100-hours, the Table 4.3 costs), the Epoch trends page, the Edelman et al. GPT-5 piece, METR TH1.1, the HLE leaderboard (54.80), ClockBench (83.3 / 90.7), DeepSeek-V4, Qwen3.5 and the Yue et al. wording.
3. **There is one print defect that must be fixed first.** In the typeset PDF, every token with a leading space prints with a closing quotation mark (”), not an opening one (“). It happens about 30 times across Tables 3.1 and 3.7 and the tokenisation and decoding text, which is exactly where the book tells the reader that "quotation marks show where a token includes a leading space". The fix is mechanical.
4. **Some figure captions print citations as bare numbers inside sentences.** For example "Schematic by the author, after 311, 864, 1080" and "after the power-law fits of 549". That is a build or style defect, not a content one, but a referee or copy-editor would not let it through.
5. **The prose is clear and well structured, but over-hedged.** There are 38 author-attribution tags in Ch. 3 and 23 in Ch. 4, plus repeated peer-review status qualifiers. They make otherwise strong expository prose stop and start. Cutting them is editorial work, not a precondition for publication.

So: yes, you can put these two chapters out, after fixing the quote glyphs and the caption citations, correcting the two small errors below, and ideally doing one de-hedging pass.

## 2. Blocking issues

**B1. Leading-space tokens print with the wrong quotation mark throughout the tokenisation material.**
- Source: `ch03.md:11, 15–22, 63, 84, 185, 189–193, 201, 208, 213, 250`.
- What prints: PDF p. 74, Table 3.1 shows `” in”`, `” Edinburgh”`, `” rarely”` and so on. The running text on p. 74 reads "split into ” token” and “isation”". Pages 77, 88–89 and 93 do the same: "If the true continuation is ” Paris”", and Table 3.7 shows `” the” 8.5%`.
- Problem: the smart-quote filter treats a quote followed by a space as a closing quote. The book's own key convention ("Quotation marks show where a token includes a leading space", Table 3.1 caption) is therefore printed wrongly about 30 times. A reader sees what looks like a typo in every one of them, in the section meant to teach tokenisation.
- Evidence: `pdftotext -f 63 -l 134` finds 31 lines matching `” [token]”` in the Ch. 3 pages. See also the rendered p. 74.
- Fix: set tokens in code font with straight quotes (`" in"`), or use a visible space marker (`␣in`, or the `Ġ` convention), and turn off smart quotes for those spans. Then re-check Tables 3.1 and 3.7, Section 3.4 and the "Going deeper" box.

**B2. Caption citations render as bare numbers inside sentences.**
- Where:
  - Fig 3.1, p. 78: "illustrating findings of 704, 705"
  - Fig 3.2, p. 83: "Schematic by the author, after 311, 864, 1080; probabilities computed … settings as in Table 3.7 864."
  - Fig 3.3, p. 93: "based on 99, 453, 819; share of computation from 277, 453"
  - Fig 4.1, p. 110: "after the power-law fits of 549 and the irreducible-loss form used by 477, 793"
- Problem: narrative `@key` citations in captions become numbers that act as nouns in the sentence, which is ungrammatical. In Fig. 3.2 a source number also runs straight on after "Table 3.7". Body text renders the same keys as names ("Kalai et al. make this precise"), so captions are inconsistent with the text.
- Fix: in captions, write the names out ("after Vaswani et al. (2017) [1080]"), or restrict captions to a final "Source: n, n" line. This is probably a book-wide build fix.

## 3. Major issues

**M1. Wrong date for the ARC-AGI cost revision.**
- Where: `ch04.md:179`.
- Text: "Twelve points thus cost roughly 175 times more per task, by the benchmark organisers' retail-price estimates, revised in March 2025."
- Problem: the $26 and $4,560 figures are the 10 December 2025 revision, which switched to o3-pro pricing of $80 per million tokens. The March 2025 revision used o1-pro pricing and gave much higher costs. You can check this on the page itself: 33.5M tokens × $80/M = $2,680, which is the table's "Retail Cost" for the $26-per-task run.
- Evidence: https://arcprize.org/blog/oai-o3-pub-breakthrough. The page carries two notes, "Note 3/24/2025: … o1-pro pricing" and "Note 12/10/2025: o3 pricing costs have been updated to use o3-pro pricing of $80/M/Tokens".
- Fix: change to "revised in December 2025". Optionally add that the absolute figures have been revised twice, which is itself a lesson about cost claims.

**M2. Statistic mislabelled for GSM1k.**
- Where: `ch04.md:203`.
- Text: "correlated with its performance gap (Spearman rank correlation 0.36)".
- Problem: the paper reports "Spearman's r^2 = 0.36", which is a squared coefficient (ρ ≈ 0.6), not a correlation of 0.36. The book understates the relationship.
- Evidence: arXiv:2405.00332 abstract.
- Fix: write "(Spearman's r² = 0.36)", or "a moderate positive rank correlation".

**M3. The position-information argument is technically overstated.**
- Where: `ch03.md:171`.
- Text: "shuffling those tokens leaves the output unchanged … The causal mask only decides which tokens are visible; it does not say where each one sits. Position information is therefore supplied explicitly".
- Problem: for a single attention layer over a fixed visible set this is true. A stacked causal decoder with no position encoding is not order-blind, though, because the mask breaks permutation symmetry and models learn position implicitly. See Haviv et al. 2022, "Transformer Language Models without Positional Encodings Still Learn Positional Information", and Kazemnejad et al. 2023 (NeurIPS). A specialist reviewer will flag this.
- Fix: say the attention operation itself is order-blind, and that explicit encodings are used because they work far better, with a one-clause nod to NoPE results.

**M4. Too much formalism in places for an educated non-specialist.**
- Where: Ch. 3, the DPO loss (`:278`). Ch. 4, the Kaplan combined law (`:47`) and the description of Chinchilla's Approach 1 (`:59`).
- Problem: each is correct, but readers have to wade through log-ratios and exponent-of-exponent formulas that add nothing the surrounding words don't already say. The book already has "Going deeper" boxes.
- Fix: move the DPO equation, the L(N, D) equation and the Approach 1/2/3 mechanics into boxes. Keep the verbal explanation and Table 4.1 in the main text.

**M5. Too many attribution tags and status qualifiers.**
- Examples:
  - "(author computation, tiktoken 0.14.0)"
  - "by the author's arithmetic"
  - "in the author's view"
  - "Anthropic researchers, in unreviewed work published on the company's research site"
  - "a design taken over from GPT-1, which OpenAI also described in a report that was not peer-reviewed" (`ch03.md:144`)
  - "In a follow-up study, also unreviewed and from Anthropic"
- Counts: "author" appears 38× in Ch. 3 and 23× in Ch. 4. The "author's view/reading" framings appear 5× and 8×.
- Problem: the transparency is admirable, but the cumulative effect is defensive and breaks the reading rhythm.
- Fix: add one methods note at chapter start, along the lines of "Unless stated otherwise, numbers marked † were computed by the author; software versions are listed in the notes". Then use a symbol or drop the tags. State peer-review status once per source type, or move it to the source line. Keep "in the author's view" only where it marks a genuine judgement, such as the scaling-debate conclusions.

## 4. Minor issues

- **`ch03.md:322`, GRPO worked example.** "the standard deviation 0.5, so the correct answers are reinforced with +1 and the wrong ones … −1". That is true only with the population standard deviation. Common implementations (for example TRL's `std()`) use the sample standard deviation, giving about ±0.87. Say "roughly +1 and −1".
- **`ch03.md:322`, GRPO and PPO.** GRPO "is a variant of PPO that keeps its safeguards … including a penalty for drifting from a reference model". The KL penalty is not part of PPO itself; it comes from the RLHF set-up (InstructGPT). GRPO keeps the clipping and puts the KL term in the loss. Rephrase.
- **`ch03.md:177`, DeepSeek-V3 routing.** "each token is routed to 8 of 256 experts per layer". It also always uses 1 shared expert, and the 37B active parameters include it. Add "plus one shared expert".
- **`ch03.md:98`, dot product.** "gives a large number when both have large values in the same places". Strictly it needs large values of the same sign. Add "with the same sign".
- **`ch04.md:209`, HLE launch score.** "At release in early 2025 the best model tested scored 13.4%". Could not verify this framing. At the January 2025 launch the leading text-only scores were around 9% (o1, DeepSeek-R1). 13.4% is a later o3-mini (high) entry in the paper's revised versions. Consider "In the published paper, the best model scored 13.4%".
- **`ch04.md:215`, Claude Mythos Preview.** "measured about 17 hours". METR's headline is "at least 16 hours" (95% CI 8.5–55 h). About 17.4 h appears only in the data file. Say "at least 16 hours (about 17 in METR's data file)".
- **`ch04.md:149`, Sutskever.** "formerly of Google and OpenAI" omits his current role as co-founder of Safe Superintelligence, which is relevant context for his "age of research" claim.
- **`ch03.md:136` / PDF p. 83, punctuation.** "introduced for image networks by He et al.." has a double full stop because the citation is followed by a sentence end.
- **`ch03.md:185`, token spelling.** "GPT-2 small gives 'Paris' only 3.2%" has no leading space, while the table and the surrounding text use `" Paris"`. Make it consistent with the convention you teach two sections earlier.
- **Typography.** Inline code (`o200k_base`, `tiktoken`) prints noticeably larger and heavier than body text (p. 74). Scale the monospace font to the x-height of the body font.
- **Table 3.8, PDF pp. 90–91.** The table splits across pages and its last row is separated from the header. Keep it together, or repeat the header.
- **Captions repeat the text.** Table 3.1's caption repeats the paragraph above it almost word for word, and Table 3.4's and Fig. 3.2's captions are long. Cut captions to what the reader needs to read the exhibit.
- **`ch03.md:246`, Fig. 3.3.** "about 5% for DeepSeek-R1". The denominator (2.79M GPU-hours) includes V3's context extension and post-training. Against pre-training alone (2.664M) it is about 5.5%. Harmless, but say "about 5% of DeepSeek-V3's total reported training".
- **Fig. 4.2.** Grok 3 and GPT-4.5 share one label and almost one marker. Readers can't tell there are two points, and the text counts 13. Offset or separate them.
- **Currency.** Several "as of October 2026" leaderboard figures will date quickly: ClockBench, the HLE top entry and METR's "no longer actively updated" page. Consider moving them into a dated box, or into a single "state of play, October 2026" paragraph per chapter.

## 5. What works

- Real, reproducible examples. The tokeniser tables, BPE trace, hand-checkable attention table, GPT-2 parameter ledger, KV-cache table and base-model next-token table are excellent teaching devices, and all the ones I could rerun are exact.
- The technical explanations of tokenisation, attention and masking, the residual stream, KV cache and GQA, RLHF, DPO and RLVR/GRPO are correct and pitched well. The causal-mask paragraph that distinguishes encoder from decoder, and explains why the Fig. 3.2 inset can look ahead, is a nice touch.
- Ch. 4 has a strong arc: FLOP accounting, Kaplan, Chinchilla and its correction (Besiroglu, with the recomputed losses), over-training, then trends, data, test-time compute, measurement, and finally slowdown. It handles uncertainty and estimate confidence (Epoch's labels) honestly.
- The debates are fair and evidence-based: emergence, understanding, and whether scaling is slowing. The closing syntheses are crisp.
- Sourcing is current and primary. Most 2026 claims trace to Epoch's CSV notes or the original pages.
