# Second review: Preface, Chapter 1 and Chapter 2

Reviewer scope: `textbook/chapters/ch00-preface.md` (54 lines), `ch01.md` (287 lines), `ch02.md` (356 lines). I read every line, along with the bib entries for recent sources, the SVG labels of Figures 1.1–1.3 and 2.1–2.6, and the rendered PDF pages 7–72 (figure pages 19, 30, 36 and 65 viewed as images). ## 1. Verdict

**Publish after minor fixes.**

These are the reasons that decide it:

1. **The facts are accurate.** I found no sourced claim that is wrong. That covers 46 checks, weighted towards 2025–26 material and towards numbers and quotations. Quotations are exact, numbers match, and page and paragraph pinpoints were right wherever I could open the source (Commission guidelines paras 7, 39, 41, 42, 48, 62, 63; MYCIN pp. 215–216, 255–260; Haigh 2024; Zech 2018; Seide 2011; DeepSeek-V4; Kimi K2). This is unusually careful work.
2. **The technical content is correct and well taught.** The history of AI (symbolic AI, the winters, the statistical turn, deep learning) and the neural-network fundamentals (neuron, XOR, loss, gradient descent, backpropagation, momentum, Adam/AdamW, initialisation, normalisation, residuals, convolution, generalisation, double descent) are sound. Every worked example recomputes exactly: softmax, the activation table, the learning-rate divergence threshold, the nine-parameter backprop update, Adam's first step, He initialisation, the convolution and pooling figure, and H100 arithmetic intensity.
3. **The prose is good but carries visible machinery.** Chapter 2 attributes its own arithmetic 20 times ("worked example by the author", "Calculation by the author", "by the author's arithmetic"). The two Debate boxes end with the same template sentence ("In the author's view, settling the question needs …"), which also appears in Ch. 8. Several "In the author's view" labels are attached to trivially true statements. This is what makes the text read as machine-assembled, and it is cheap to fix.
4. **Chapter 1 has some structural lumps.** The subsection "Reasoning models and agents" opens with an adoption-statistics paragraph. A neural-network history paragraph interrupts the "More data, simpler models" argument. The OSWorld sentence chains three numbers whose comparability is unclear.
5. **Nothing blocks publication.** All the issues below are editorial or nuance fixes, a few hours of work.

## 3. Major issues (fix before publication; not factual errors)

**M1. Self-attribution clutter (ch02 throughout; ch01:88, 220; ch00:44).**
Chapter 2 contains 34 occurrences of "author", 20 of them attributing arithmetic. Examples: "In a worked example by the author" (ch02:13, 53, 92, 106), "Calculation by the author" (Tables 2.1–2.3, Figs 2.1–2.3, 2.5), "By the author's arithmetic" (ch02:188, 241, 255, 259, 336), "Applied by the author to the one-weight example" (ch02:180).
- **Problem:** the preface already says that numbers marked as computed by the author are the author's (ch00:44). Repeating it in nearly every paragraph is distracting and reads as defensive boilerplate.
- **Fix:** state once in the preface that all worked examples and unlabelled arithmetic are the author's. Keep the label only in figure and table captions (one short "Calculation by the author"), and delete it from running prose.

**M2. Formulaic Debate-box endings and over-used opinion labels.**
- ch01:251: "In the author's view, settling the question needs a capability that keeps resisting scale … which would favour Marcus, or one … that emerges from scale alone, which would favour Sutton."
- ch02:345: "In the author's view, settling the question needs direct measurements …". The identical frame also appears at ch08:175.
- **Problem:** the same sentence frame closing every Debate box is a recognisable generation pattern. Reviewers and readers will notice it.
- **Fix:** vary or drop it. For example, in Ch. 1: "Two kinds of result would settle it: …"

Low-value labels to delete:
- ch01:29 "often disagree, in the author's view, about the target, not the evidence". The parenthetical breaks the sentence; move it to the front or drop it.
- ch01:262 "In the author's view, this is one reason why spam filters …"
- ch01:278 "In the author's view, figures on AI adoption … depend on which systems are counted". This is a truism and needs no label.
- ch02:70 "which is, in the author's summary, the job hidden layers do".

Keep the labels where a real judgement is made (ch01:270 on Suchman, ch02:156 on synapses vs parameters, ch02:168, ch02:259).

**M3. Chapter 1 §1.7 structure (ch01:232–238).**
- **Problem:** the subsection headed "Reasoning models and agents" opens with an adoption paragraph (Bick et al., the St. Louis Fed blog, Microsoft telemetry) that has nothing to do with reasoning models or agents. Adoption figures for ChatGPT are also scattered across ch01:5, :224 and :234.
- **Fix:** move the adoption paragraph to the end of "Assistants, multimodal and open models", directly after the 700-million figure, or into §1.8.

Also ch01:161, the paragraph beginning "Through the 1990s the field's centre of gravity moved …":
- **Problem:** it switches mid-paragraph into a neural-network chronology (Hopfield 1982, backprop 1986, LSTM 1997, LeNet, Hinton 2006). That interrupts the data-over-algorithms argument, which resumes with Banko and Brill at ch01:163.
- **Fix:** split it into its own short paragraph or subsection ("Neural networks underground").

**M4. OSWorld sentence chain (ch01:238).**
The text reads: "people completed about 72% of tasks and the best model about 12% [@xie2024osworld]. By early 2026, according to Stanford's annual AI Index report, the best agent reached 66.3% [@sajadieh2026ai]. On the official leaderboard for a corrected task set, OSWorld-Verified, whose scores are not directly comparable with the original, the best general-purpose model reached about 86% by August 2026."
- **What checks out:** the AI Index does say 66.3% (sajadieh2026ai, §2.6: "Claude Opus 4.5 leading on accuracy on OSWorld with 66.3%", sourced from Epoch AI). The leaderboard file does show a top general model at 85.96% dated end-July 2026.
- **Problem:** the 66.3% is Anthropic's reported Opus 4.5 figure, which trackers list as an OSWorld-Verified score, not a score on the original 2024 task set. If so, the caveat "not directly comparable" is attached to the wrong pair. The real non-comparable pair is the 2024 12% versus both later figures.
- **Status:** I could not fully settle which task set the AI Index's Epoch-sourced number uses.
- **Fix:** check Epoch's OSWorld series, then say plainly which set each figure refers to. Alternatively, drop the 66.3% and give 12% (2024, original) and about 86% (2026, Verified) with a single caveat.

## 4. Minor issues

**Chapter 1**
- **ch01:90** "in 1967 Minsky forecast …" is attributed "As quoted by Melanie Mitchell", but Mitchell gives no year ("a few years later", arXiv:2104.12871 p. 2). Either cite Minsky (1967) directly or write "in the late 1960s".
- **ch01:88** The aside "an understatement, since by the author's arithmetic 10^120 microseconds is about 3 × 10^106 years" is correct, but it is pedantic and invites a fight over printings: some versions of Shannon's paper read "micro-micro-second". Cut it, or move it to a footnote.
- **ch01:169** IBM's "32 processors" conflicts with the builders' own description: "a 30-node (30-processor) IBM RS/6000 SP … and 480 chess chips" (Campbell, Hoane & Hsu 2002, *AIJ* 134, §§2, 4). Since the text quotes IBM marketing, add the builders' figure or drop the number.
- **ch01:169, :260** The text says Deep Blue "applies predefined rules without learning from data". Campbell et al. 2002 §7.3 say "the large majority" of the evaluation weights were hand-tuned, but some (pawn shelter) were tuned by comparison training on game data before the 1997 match. The "may fall outside" hedge is fine. Add half a sentence so the book is not seen to endorse a factual over-simplification.
- **ch01:226** GPT-4 "passed a simulated bar exam with a score around the top 10%" is properly attributed to the company. Given the preface's promise to show evidence strength, note that independent re-analysis judged this percentile overstated (Martínez 2024, *Artificial Intelligence and Law*); it is not in the bib.
- **ch01:234** Bick et al. "has outpaced both PCs and the internet". I could confirm the substance in the NBER version (overall adoption faster) but not the exact wording in the *Management Science* version. The same paper finds workplace adoption tracking PCs, so the overall speed is driven by home use. Add "overall" or that nuance.
- **ch01:177 and Table 1.3** The text describes the result as "Switchboard". Seide et al.'s 27.4%→18.5% is on the RT03S Fisher subset (their own abstract also says "(Switchboard)"). This is acceptable, but "a standard telephone-speech test set" is safer.
- **Figure 1.2 (PDF p. 30)** The label "One annotator, 1,500 images (5.1%)" sits near the 15% gridline, far above the dashed line at 5.1%. A reader may misread it. Move the label onto the line.
- **Figure 1.3 (PDF p. 36)**
  - "MYCIN evaluation (1979)" sits in the "Deployment and policy" column. It was a research study, and the chapter stresses that MYCIN was never deployed. Move it to the research side.
  - The "Statistical ML" era band starts around 1990, while the text dates the statistical turn to 1976 (Jelinek).
  - The caption's 34-item source list is unwieldy in print.
- **Bib `bastian2026openai`** The title "OpenAI Revenue Keeps Surging as Company Seeks $30 Billion…" does not match the URL slug or the page headline as fetched ("ChatGPT now reaches 1.2 billion people every week, OpenAI says"). The figure itself is confirmed (DevDay, 29 Sept 2026). Check that the title is the one at the URL.

**Chapter 2**
- **ch02:5** The opening carries a heavy provenance clause: "like DeepSeek-V4-Pro, an open model whose developer described a 'preview version' in a 2026 company technical report, stores 1.6 trillion adjustable numbers". It is stilted in a chapter opener. Keep the number here and move the provenance to §2.2 (ch02:78), where the model reappears.
- **ch02:21** "A jump has no slope" is imprecise. A step function has zero slope everywhere except at the jump, where the slope is undefined. The problem is that the gradient is zero almost everywhere. Rephrase.
- **ch02:188** "A fixed standard deviation of 0.01, as used in AlexNet, gives a factor … 0.0256 per layer, so after ten such layers …" implies that AlexNet's signal vanished. AlexNet had 8 layers of varying width and trained fine. Mark the 512-input, ten-layer case as hypothetical.
- **ch02:225 (Llama bullet)** "RMSNorm applied at the input of each sub-layer … an arrangement used earlier in OpenAI's GPT-2". GPT-2 used pre-normalisation with LayerNorm, not RMSNorm, and the LLaMA paper itself credits GPT-3. Write "pre-normalisation (with LayerNorm) was used earlier in GPT-2".
- **ch02:225–228 (frontier recipes box)** The DeepSeek-V4 bullet should say that the report uses Muon for "the majority of modules", with AdamW retained elsewhere. As written, it implies Muon throughout.
- **ch02:3** LeNet "millions of bank cheques a month" matches LeCun et al. 1998 §I, but the same paper's abstract says "several million checks per day". This is fine as written; the inconsistency is in the source.

**Preface**
- **ch00:5** "about a thousand references": the bib has 1,160 unique keys and the PDF list runs to at least 1,137. Write "more than 1,100".
- **ch00:3** "what it is already doing to work, science, media and public life". On first read "doing to work" parses as a verb phrase. Use "to employment" or "to jobs".
- **ch00:38** Company technical reports are treated "as reliable descriptions of the company's own methods". This overstates: the book itself flags contested DeepSeek cost claims (Ch. 7). Write "generally taken at face value for their methods".
- **ch00:52** "Every factual claim was checked against the source cited for it". For this scope my checks support the claim. Keep it, but the author should be confident it holds for all 14 chapters.

## 5. What works

- **Opening.** Chapter 1 opens with a hook (the funding request) and closes its loop well. The legal-definition thread, with the guidelines putting MYCIN inside and Deep Blue possibly outside, is original and genuinely illuminating.
- **Case studies.** The MYCIN case study (CF arithmetic, the brittleness example at 0.999→0.199, the blinded 1979 study, why it was abandoned) is first-rate, and every number checks against Buchanan & Shortliffe.
- **Worked examples.** Chapter 2's worked examples teach the mechanism in numbers a non-specialist can follow. The highlights are the one-weight learning-rate divergence (η > 0.25), the nine-parameter backprop with its numerical-gradient check, and Adam's step size bounded by α. All are exactly right.
- **Hedging is honest and calibrated.** Examples include Haigh's dissent on the first winter, the single-annotator caveat on "superhuman" ImageNet, Santurkar's challenge to covariate shift, and Zech's own admission that they "cannot fully assess" other factors.
- **Tables 2.4 and 2.5** are accurate and genuinely useful. Figures 2.3, 2.4 and 2.5 are correct in every label I checked (for example, pooling output 4, 2, 2, 5 and the descent points 0.44→0.488→0.4976).
