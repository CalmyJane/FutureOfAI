# Second independent review: Chapters 7 and 8

Reviewer scope: `textbook/chapters/ch07.md` (chips, data centres, energy, open models, compute governance) and `textbook/chapters/ch08.md` (work and economy). I read both chapters in full, along with their figures in the built PDF (pp. 199–262 of `delivery/understanding-ai_A5_interior.pdf`). I checked claims against primary sources wherever I could reach them: SEC EDGAR filings, Federal Register and govinfo, the IEA *Key Questions* PDF, Census BTOS microdata, Crossref, NBER, BLS, the Fed and the papers themselves. Review date: 3 November 2026.

---

## 1. Verdict

**Publish after minor fixes.** The fixes include two items I would treat as blocking, but both are quick to correct.

The reasons that decide it:

1. **Accuracy is unusually high.** I checked 66 claims and found no number that is verifiably wrong. This held for the numbers that are easiest to get wrong: Nvidia's revenue by headquarters location, the TPP arithmetic, IEA annex tables, capex from four 10-Ks, BTOS waves, ADP "canaries" figures, Acemoglu's term-by-term arithmetic, and METR's sign conventions. The chapters' own calculations (roofline ridge point, KV/bandwidth latency, DeepSeek MFU, Elo-to-win-rate, TPP and density) are correct.
2. **One material claim is out of date and is used as evidence.** The Nvidia–OpenAI "$100 billion" commitment (ch07:236) was shelved in early 2026 and replaced by a $30 billion equity stake. The debate box still presents it as live.
3. **Several statements will expire within days of publication.** The ownership-rule suspension (9 November 2026), the truce (10 November), and China's gallium and germanium suspension (27 November) are written as present-tense facts. As printed, the book will be wrong in its first month on sale.
4. **There are currency gaps in the energy evidence.** The LBNL *2025 Update* (June 2026) and the IEA's *Powering Artificial Intelligence* (14 October 2026) are both missing, although the book claims to be current to late October 2026. Neither omission makes the text wrong, but an expert reader will notice both.
5. **Balance, clarity and structure are strong.** Sources are well labelled as company claims, preprints or working papers. Debates are argued from both sides. The writing is dense but clean. The figures are accurate and well captioned.

## 2. Blocking issues

**B1. The Nvidia–OpenAI "$100 billion" commitment is stale and misleading.**
- Location: ch07.md:236.
- Quote: "In September 2025 Nvidia said it intended to invest up to $100 billion in OpenAI 'progressively as each gigawatt is deployed' of Nvidia systems [@nvidia2025openai], an arrangement in which the supplier's money returns as orders."
- Problem: the letter of intent was never finalised. In January 2026 Huang called it non-binding. Reuters and the FT reported on 19–20 February 2026 that Nvidia would instead take a roughly $30 billion equity stake in OpenAI's ~$110 billion round, which closed at the end of February 2026. On 4 March 2026 Huang said a $100 billion investment was "probably not in the cards". The book uses the $100 billion figure as live evidence of supplier-financed demand.
- Evidence: https://www.cp24.com/news/money/2026/02/20/nvidia-close-to-investing-us30-billion-in-openais-mega-funding-round-reuters-source-says/ ; https://news.bloomberglaw.com/private-equity/nvidias-jensen-huang-rules-out-100-billion-openai-investment
- Fix: "In September 2025 Nvidia announced a letter of intent to invest up to $100 billion in OpenAI as gigawatts of Nvidia systems were deployed. The plan was not finalised; in February 2026 Nvidia instead took a stake of about $30 billion in OpenAI's funding round." The circular-financing point still stands, and is arguably stronger. Cite a primary source (OpenAI or Nvidia announcement, or Nvidia's 10-Q).

**B2. Export-control and truce dates expire within weeks of printing.**
- Location: ch07.md:125, ch07.md:129, and the Figure 7.2 caption.
- Quote (ch07:125): "Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said in September 2026 that the truce would be extended to 10 January … the suspension of the ownership rule was due to lapse on 9 November 2026 unless extended."
- Quote (ch07:129): "it suspended that ban in November 2025, until November 2026".
- Problem: all three dates (9 November, 10 November, 27 November 2026) fall in the book's first weeks on sale. As of 3 November, BIS had not published the amendment extending the Affiliates-rule suspension. Commentators note that the announced extension is not legally effective until BIS acts (Export Compliance Daily, 25 September 2026; cmtradelaw.com, September 2026). "10 January" also lacks its year (2027).
- Fix:
  - Rewrite both passages as dated snapshots, for example "As of late October 2026, the US had announced…; BIS had not yet published…".
  - Add "2027" to the truce date.
  - Re-check on the day of final proof. If the deadlines pass before printing, replace them with the outcome.

## 3. Major issues

**M1. The newer LBNL update is missing.**
- Location: ch07.md:153.
- Quote: "An independent US estimate comes from … LBNL, whose December 2024 report … For 2028 the report gives a range of roughly 325 to 580 TWh."
- Problem: LBNL published *United States Data Center Energy Usage Report: 2025 Update* in June 2026 (Smith, …, Shehabi). Its 2030 Reference Case is 649 TWh, or 11.8% of US electricity, with sensitivity bounds of 9.5–15.3% and 521–843 TWh. The book presents the 2024 report as the current independent estimate, and the Further reading list repeats it.
- Evidence: https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/publications/united-states-data-center-energy-2025
- Fix: add one or two sentences with the 2030 figure and range. Keep the 2023 baseline and the inference share from the 2024 report if the update does not supersede them.

**M2. The IEA's October 2026 report is missing.**
- Location: ch07.md:139.
- Quote: "The most systematic global estimates come from the IEA … in its April 2025 special report … and its April 2026 update".
- Problem: the IEA published *Powering Artificial Intelligence* on 14 October 2026 (PDF CreationDate 2026-10-14), prepared for France's G7 presidency. It reports that nearly 40% of identified AI-dedicated data-centre capacity is in areas of high or extremely high baseline water stress. That finding fits directly with the water discussion at ch07:198 (Rosa et al.), and the report gives an updated capex figure of more than $700 billion. The author's own news sweep is dated 25 October.
- Evidence: https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/cd360a30-aaef-43cd-84e6-a125c3a0bc67/PoweringArtificialIntelligence.pdf
- Fix: mention the report in §7.4 and add its water-stress share alongside the Rosa et al. finding.

**M3. The Aghion and Bunel input is described confusingly.**
- Location: ch08.md:202.
- Quote: "taking the customer-support gain at its later, higher level of about 25% rather than the 14% Acemoglu used".
- Problem: elsewhere the chapter gives the customer-support study's *later* (QJE) average as 15% (Table 8.3, ch08:96, ch08:119). A reader cannot reconcile "later, higher … 25%" with "later version … 15%". The 25% is presumably the effect after several months of access, not the paper's later headline average. I could not open the Aghion–Bunel note to confirm.
- Fix: state exactly what the 25% is, for example "the gain after several months of use reported in the study". Verify it against the note (FRBSF/Collège de France PDF).

**M4. Small-modular-reactor "agreements" are presented without the IEA's caveat.**
- Location: ch07.md:184.
- Quote: "Agreements for small modular reactor capacity for data centres had reached about 45 GW by January 2026".
- Problem: the IEA (Key Questions, p. 64) stresses that these 45 GW are "largely … less formal off-take arrangements … not yet backed by firm financing commitments". Without that caveat, the figure reads as contracted supply. It sits next to the book's own warning that interconnection queues are a poor guide to what gets built.
- Fix: add a half-sentence noting that most of these are non-binding off-take arrangements.

## 4. Minor issues

- **ch07:135, DOJ case.** Quote: "a Houston company had pleaded guilty to exporting at least $160 million worth". DOJ says "exported or attempted to export". The plea was entered on 10 October 2025 and announced on 8 December 2025. Fix: "exporting or attempting to export".
- **ch07:21, latency arithmetic.** Quote: "spread over two GPUs, it takes about 21 milliseconds". This holds only with tensor parallelism; with pipeline parallelism a single request still takes about 42 ms. The phrase "however many arithmetic units sit idle" is awkward; "regardless of how many…" reads better. Add "if the weights are split so that both GPUs read in parallel".
- **ch07:41, awkward sentence.** Quote: "The limitation is shared by the H100 and is not a product of export controls, although their chip, the H800, was …". This is hard to parse. Suggested rewrite: "This limitation is a property of all Hopper GPUs, not of the export-compliant H800 that DeepSeek used (the H800 could be sold to China until the 2023 rule change)."
- **ch07:186, Google electricity growth.** Quote: "its data centres' electricity use grew by 38% in 2025". The report's running text says *total* electricity consumption grew 37%; 38% appears only in a chart. Confirm which figure the 38% is, and add a page reference.
- **ch07:66, Huawei share.** Quote: "Huawei a small but growing share". I could not find this in the AI Index (secondary coverage mentions only Nvidia, Google and Amazon). Check it against AI Index ch. 1.
- **ch07:281, LMArena gap.** The AI Index 2026 numbers (174 → 7 → 49 points) could not be verified. Secondary coverage cites a 39-point US–China gap in March 2026, which is a different comparison but could confuse readers. Add the exact page.
- **ch07:216 and Table 7.4.** "four largest US cloud and platform companies" is fine, but Microsoft's change is +79.6%, which rounds to +80% (acceptable). Consider noting that Microsoft's figures are fiscal years, which the table already does, and that Oracle and the neo-clouds are excluded.
- **ch07:94 and ch07:180, IEA paraphrase.** "From the second half of 2025, memory became the tighter limit". The IEA says "In the second half of 2025 and beginning of 2026". Fine as is.
- **ch08:154, OECD sourcing.** The *Employment Outlook 2026* is cited through *Journal du Net*. For an OECD flagship report, cite the OECD directly (in English) and keep the press report only if it adds something.
- **ch08:156 and ch08:221, imminent data releases.** The Challenger October 2026 report (due about 5 November) and the BLS Q3 2026 preliminary productivity release (due early November) will appear before printing. Re-check them at final proof.
- **ch08:7, repetition.** "along a chain that runs … It runs from …" repeats "runs".
- **ch08:202 and Table 8.4, unverified input.** The 60% exposed-task input for Aghion–Bunel could not be verified directly. It is consistent with their 0.68 pp result (0.6 × 0.5 × 0.4 × 0.57 ≈ 6.8%).
- **Layout, Table 7.3.** The caption sits at the foot of p. 215 and the table on p. 216.
- **Layout, Table 8.3.** The table begins on p. 243 with a single row before breaking. Keep each caption with its table and avoid one-row orphans.
- **Figure 8.2, year label.** Noy & Zhang is labelled "(RCT; n=453, 2022)". Confirm the data-collection year; ChatGPT was released on 30 November 2022, and most sources date the experiment to early 2023.
- **ch07, length.** The chapter is very long and §7.6 (open weights) reads almost as a separate chapter. Consider moving the GPT-NeoX/BLOOM and licence detail (Table 7.5) to a box so the main thread stays on infrastructure and power.

## 5. What works

- **Expert-level technical exposition.** The chapter keeps dense and sparse figures apart, separates precision formats, explains roofline intensity, and points out the unlike comparison between Ironwood and El Capitan. Readers rarely get this level of care on chip specifications.
- **Export-control history.** The account is precise: the 2022 two-test rule, the 2023 TPP and density thresholds, the H20 bandwidth language, the diffusion rule, the enforcement-only-for-D:1/D:4/D:5 posture and the UAE rule. All of it matches the Federal Register text.
- **Energy section.** It is model work in handling uncertainty: market-based versus location-based emissions, system boundaries for per-query energy, and scenario ranges presented as ranges.
- **Chapter 8's exposure → adoption → experiment → firm → macro chain.** It is an excellent organising device. The debate box on entry-level hiring is genuinely balanced: ADP, résumé data and freelance evidence against the Danish nulls, Fairlie, EIG and the St. Louis Fed.
- **Labelling of sources.** Company, preprint and working-paper status is consistently labelled, including Anthropic's own research, which is appropriate given that it is a party to the debate.
