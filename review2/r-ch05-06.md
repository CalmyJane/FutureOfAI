# Second review: Chapters 5 (agents) and 6 (science, mathematics, engineering)

Reviewer scope: `textbook/chapters/ch05.md` (322 lines, ~10,700 words) and `textbook/chapters/ch06.md` (303 lines, ~11,200 words). I read every line, the bib files and figure specs, and rendered the figure and table pages from `delivery/understanding-ai_A5_interior.pdf`: pp. 136, 141–142, 145, 150, 167, 170, 176, 184 and 188. I checked 50 load-bearing claims against primary sources or the best available secondary ones, weighted towards 2025–26 material. Date of review: 3 November 2026.

## 1. Verdict

**Publish after minor fixes. The list is short, but it is not optional: one print defect and four content updates must be made before release.**

The reasons that decide it:

1. **The facts are unusually accurate.** Of 50 spot-checks, 45 were confirmed. Many of them are fragile 2026 numbers: the MCP 2026-07-28 changes, the METR YAML values, the SWE-bench Pro tables, the A-Lab correction, Kosmos, MathArena and the NHC verification. I found one claim verified wrong and one imprecise. The remaining three could not be verified, and none of those looks doubtful.
2. **Balance between vendor claims and independent evidence is a real strength.** Both chapters consistently label who measured what ("company claim", "developers' own evaluations", "not independently audited"). They pair each showcase result with an outside test: Terwilliger, Cheetham and Seshadri, Leeman, Juelsholt, Beel, Si, the NHC report, and Petrov/MathArena.
3. **The currency claim ("current to late October 2026") does not hold for two headline developments**, and the gaps distort the picture in each chapter:
   - Chapter 6 omits OpenAI's May 2026 disproof of Erdős's unit-distance conjecture. Mathematicians treated it as the first AI result of independent mathematical interest. Without it, the chapter's framing ("long tail ... straightforward solutions", 1–2%) understates the state of the art.
   - Chapter 5 omits OpenClaw/Moltbook (January–March 2026), the most widely reported agent deployment and agent-security story of the year. Its absence makes one sentence (ch05:297) now arguably false.
4. **The Navier–Stokes case study, the chapter's showpiece, is accurate in what it says but incomplete in a way that matters.** It omits the public priority dispute (Buckmaster and Alpöge against OpenAI) and the Clay Institute's 11 September statement. It also misstates the claim as "one of the four alternatives" when the manuscript claims two.
5. **A typesetting defect in the printed book:** both JSON code blocks in §5.3 run off the right margin and are clipped (p. 141 "for a loc…", p. 142 "Current weather in Leed…"). A reader cannot be sent a book with truncated code.

## 2. Blocking issues

**B1. Code blocks clipped in print (ch05:71–98; PDF pp. 141–142).**
- Problem: the tool definition line `"description": "Get current weather information for a location",` prints as `…for a loc` and is cut at the trim. The `"description": "City name or zip code"` line touches the edge. On p. 142 the result line `"text": "Current weather in Leeds: ..."` prints as `…in Leed`. `pdftotext -layout` confirms the truncation (line 19: `"Get current weather information for a loc`).
- Fix: reflow the JSON to at most about 60 monospace characters per line (break the description string, or shorten it to "Current weather for a place"), or set code at a smaller size. Then re-render pp. 141–142, and check every other code block in the book for the same fault.

**B2. Navier–Stokes case study: misstated claim, priority dispute missing, Clay statement missing (ch06:217–223).**
- (a) The text says the manuscript "states that this establishes one of the four alternatives". This is **verified wrong.** The OpenAI manuscript (p. 1, `cdn.openai.com/.../navier-stokes.pdf`) says: "This establishes alternative (C) … Compact support also yields the corresponding construction on T³ … establishing alternative (D)." Fix: "states that this establishes alternatives (C) and (D) (either of which would suffice) in the Clay statement".
- (b) The box omits the priority dispute, which was the dominant story in September 2026 coverage. Tristan Buckmaster (NYU) and Levent Alpöge alleged that OpenAI took up the problem after learning of their smooth-forced Euler blow-up work (dated 15 August). OpenAI denied it ("categorically impossible"; Bubeck: "false and inflammatory"). The Fields Medallists' letter "A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics" and an AMS statement followed. Sources: SciAm, 8 Sep 2026 (https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/openai-claims-blockbuster-math-breakthrough-amid-swirl-of-controversy/); timeline at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier%E2%80%93Stokes_priority_controversy. For a book whose Chapter 6 theme is how claims are checked and credited, leaving this out is a balance failure. One or two neutral sentences under "What is contested" would fix it.
- (c) "What would settle it … a decision by the Clay Institute" reads as if Clay had said nothing. On 11 September the CMI said the problem "has apparently been settled", called its process "deliberately unhurried" and changed the problem's status to "active" (https://the-decoder.com/clay-mathematics-institute-says-the-navier-stokes-millennium-prize-problem-has-apparently-been-settled/). Fix: report the statement. Note that it is more favourable to the claim than the box's tone suggests, which shows the book is not stacking the deck.
- Note also that the Bastounis et al. critique is joined by a KCL press release (https://www.kcl.ac.uk/news/openais-navier-stokes-proof-called-into-question). The authors stress that they have not shown the natural-language proof is wrong. The box should carry that qualifier so the critique is not over-read.

**B3. Erdős section and Figure 6.3 omit the most significant AI mathematics result of 2026 (ch06:206–212; Fig. 6.3, p. 188).**
- In May 2026 an OpenAI internal model disproved Erdős's planar unit-distance conjecture (Erdős problem #90), using algebraic number theory. Will Sawin extended the result within days. Daniel Litt called it "the first result produced autonomously by an AI that I find interesting in itself", and Timothy Gowers said he would recommend it to the *Annals* "without any hesitation" had a human submitted it (ScienceAlert, 28 May 2026: https://www.sciencealert.com/stunning-ai-solution-for-80-year-old-problem-shocks-mathematicians). A DeepMind team also resolved nine lesser Erdős problems.
- The author's own log flags this as not done (`work/evidence-ch06.md:90`: "#90, May 2026, listed on Tao's wiki as resolving a major open problem … not verified further and are not added").
- As written, §6.6 tells readers that AI successes are on the "long tail" of problems with "straightforward solutions". That was true in January and was overtaken in May. Fix: add a short paragraph after #728 and a timeline entry in Fig. 6.3. Keep the caveats: it was not peer reviewed, and Sawin's improvement only bites at about 10^2,000,000 points.

**B4. Chapter 5 omits OpenClaw/Moltbook, and one sentence is now contradicted (ch05:297; §5.6, §5.8).**
- ch05:297 reads: "Because large populations of capable LLM agents interacting in the wild are still rare, the report is a structured map of mechanisms, not a measurement of current harm."
- In January–February 2026 the open-source agent OpenClaw (formerly Clawdbot/Moltbot) spawned Moltbook, a network of more than 1.5 million registered agents. Reported problems included agent-to-agent prompt injection, CVE-2026-25253 (CVSS 8.8), about 21,600 exposed instances (Censys) and a backend open to agent hijacking (404 Media). Overviews: Palo Alto Networks (https://www.paloaltonetworks.com/blog/?p=351578) and Krebs, 8 Mar 2026 (https://krebsonsecurity.com/2026/03/how-ai-assistants-are-moving-the-security-goalposts/).
- This is the year's clearest real-world instance of Willison's "lethal trifecta" (§5.6) and of uncontrolled agent populations (§5.8). Neither the word nor the case appears anywhere in the book (`grep -il openclaw\|moltbook chapters/` returns nothing).
- Fix: rewrite ch05:297, for example: "large populations of agents now interact in the wild (Moltbook, 2026), but measurements of harm are still anecdotal". Add three or four sentences to §5.6 as a second real-world case beside CVE-2025-32711. Use primary or security-firm sources, and treat the agent counts as reported, not audited.

## 3. Major issues

**M1. The METR horizon for Mythos is quoted without METR's own reliability warning (ch05:162).**
- The text says Mythos Preview "reached about 17 hours, with an interval of roughly 8 to 55 hours". The METR YAML confirms 1,044.8 min (CI 508.9–3,304.3). However, METR states that "estimates above 16 hours are unreliable with the current suite", and its doubling-time fit explicitly excludes points above 16 h (`doubling_time_in_days: # excludes points with central estimate p50 > 16 hrs`).
- Fix: add "a value METR itself regards as beyond what its task suite can reliably measure".
- Separately, Opus 4.6's "about 12 hours (5–61)" matches the current YAML (718.8 min). Many readers will remember METR's widely reported February figure of about 14.5 h (CI 6–98 h). A half-clause noting the later re-estimate would prevent "error" reports.

**M2. Chapter 5 leans heavily on Anthropic sources for its spine.**
- Anthropic material is cited 23 times from 9 distinct sources: both Project Vend posts (the opening, the closing and two middle references), "Building effective agents", context engineering, the multi-agent system, the Economic Index, MCP, and computer use. The definitional and architectural material in §5.1–5.2 (workflows versus agents, compaction, sub-agents, the Pokémon note-taking example) is almost entirely Anthropic engineering-blog content.
- The text flags vendor status well (ch05:7, ch05:25), but a referee will notice the tilt.
- Fix: add one non-Anthropic source for the workflow/agent distinction and for context management. OpenAI's or Google's agent guides would do, as would an academic survey. Consider whether the closing paragraph needs to return to Project Vend a fifth time.

**M3. Table 5.1 hides the systems behind its headline numbers (ch05:136–138).**
- The rows "Best general-purpose model | Aug 2026 | 86.0%" and "Best agentic framework | Jul 2026 | 90.2%" do not name the model or framework. A reader cannot follow up, and the vendor-versus-independent distinction the caption draws is weakened.
- Fix: name them, as the evidence log already does (85.96% on 1 Aug 2026; 90.19% on 25 Jul 2026). Also note that the OSWorld site has pointed users to OSWorld 2.0 since 26 June 2026, so the Verified leaderboard is no longer the main board.

**M4. Figure 6.1's inner-ring and outer labels muddle the chapter's own taxonomy (ch06:13; p. 167).**
- The outer tag at "Collect data" reads "Predict: stand in for data". In the text, *prediction* is learning a mapping such as structure from sequence. "Standing in for data" is Messeri and Crockett's *Surrogate* role, and in this chapter's terms closer to emulation.
- The labels are also set at about 6 pt italic at A5 size and are hard to read.
- Fix: relabel the tag to "Predict: e.g. structure from sequence" and enlarge the label type.

## 4. Minor issues

- **ch05:106** says the July 2026 revision "deprecated three older features". The changelog deprecates Roots, Sampling and Logging in one SEP. It also reclassifies HTTP+SSE and the `includeContext` values and deprecates Dynamic Client Registration. Write "deprecated several older features, among them Roots, Sampling and Logging".
- **ch05:176** cites Goodfellow et al. for the product rule for independent events. A probability textbook, or no citation, would be more natural. The same applies to the Fig. 5.3 caption.
- **ch05:200–208 (Table 5.3)** is built on Claude Opus 4.5, a model about a year old by publication. The per-domain figures are internally consistent: the mean of 84.0, 79.6 and 92.3 is 85.3, matching taubench.com. Say in the caption why an older model was used, for example "the most recent model with a full four-domain breakdown".
- **ch05:246** says "more than 500 participants and $20,000 in prizes". This is not in the arXiv abstract and I could not verify it. Check it against the full text.
- **ch05:142, line 2 of the paragraph:** "On the revised benchmark the student baseline had in fact already been passed". The caveat follows, but "in fact" overstates the comparison, since the baseline was measured on different tasks. Try "a score above the original student baseline had been reported".
- **ch06:45** says the AFDB "grew from about 300,000 predicted structures in 2021". The July 2021 release held about 365,000. Write "about 350,000" or "over 350,000".
- **ch06:61** opens with "Weather forecasting is the other field that has most of what the protein case combines". The antecedent sentence ("The protein case combines four things…") was removed in revision (`evidence-ch06.md:230`), so the phrase now dangles. Rewrite it, for example: "Weather forecasting shares the protein case's advantages: …".
- **ch06:93 and Table 6.1** reproduce NOAA's "adds 18–24 h of skill" for AIGEFS. The same NOAA release elsewhere calls AIGEFS skill "comparable to" GEFS. Flag the internal inconsistency or soften the figure.
- **ch06:167 and Table 6.2** say of Seed-Prover "one after the deadline". This is confirmed only via the author's log (PDF text). The abstract says only "fully prove 5 out of 6". It is acceptable as written; quote the source page if possible.
- **ch06:184 and Table 6.2, IMO 2026 row:** some later coverage (AIBase) says the Celia and dots-note-3.0 results were graded through the IMO's official channel. I could not confirm this from an IMO source. Recheck before printing; if they were certified, the "company claims" framing understates them.
- **ch06:194** gives AlphaEvolve's 48-multiplication 4×4 complex result "according to the company". It would be fairer to add that human researchers matched or extended such schemes within weeks (2025). This is optional.
- **ch06:287 (debate box)** says the NHC "ranked an AI ensemble with or above the official forecasts for hurricane tracks and intensity". For intensity, the NHC report says official forecasts beat all models at 12 h and 96 h and were "comparable" to GDMI elsewhere. Write "on a par with the official forecasts for intensity and slightly better for short-range tracks".
- **Bibliography:** `kapoor2025ai` is an `@article` with no journal; it appeared in TMLR 2025. `wang2023voyager` is an `@article` with year 2024 and no journal; it also appeared in TMLR 2024. Both may print incompletely.
- **Prose:** ch05 §5.4 runs as a catalogue of benchmark numbers (eight tables and percentages in about 2,500 words). It is accurate but tiring. One short summary sentence per subsection, saying what the numbers mean for a reader deciding whether to use an agent, would help.

## 5. What works

- The technical exposition is excellent and correct:
  - ReAct, Reflexion, MemGPT and the generative-agent retrieval score (0.995^24 ≈ 0.89 and 0.995^168 ≈ 0.43 check out).
  - Compounding error, including the worked recovery case 0.9975^20 ≈ 0.95.
  - The pass@k and pass^k estimators, with a correct worked example (C(6,4)/C(8,4) = 15/70).
  - The Jensen-type argument for why observed pass^k exceeds p̄^k, illustrated with real τ² numbers.
  - The AlphaFold 2 architecture (Evoformer, triangle updates, recycling) and its Figure 6.2.
  - The forecast-skill and blurring boxes.
- The MCP and A2A material is current to the 2026-07-28 MCP revision and A2A 1.0. It is verified, and the JSON examples match the specification, including `resultType: "complete"`.
- The materials section, which runs from the GNoME, A-Lab and MatterGen claims through the reanalyses and the January 2026 correction to Juelsholt 2026, is a model of how a popular book should treat a contested "discovery" claim.
- §6.8 (AI-scientist systems) and Table 6.3 rest on independent evaluations rather than vendor claims, and they report the denominators.
- The debate boxes are genuinely two-sided and end with what evidence would settle the question.
- The prose is clear and calm, with few clichés. Author's judgements are consistently flagged ("in the author's view").
