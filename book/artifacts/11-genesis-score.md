# Genesis Score: final report
*When Everything Goes Right* (working title) · 2026-10-20

## 1. Where the book stands
The manuscript is complete: a prologue, 17 chapters, an epilogue and an appendix, about 82,000 words, every planned chapter drafted, audited and revised three times. Blind simulated readers keep reading every sampled chapter, and they remember its scenes and coined ideas. They still lose attention wherever the book turns into specification or recap. On the strict internal scale (raw scores minus 0.8), the book sits around 7.3 against a target of 8.5. The weakest areas are Market (unverified comps, a split general/researcher audience) and Pacing (the same chapter template throughout). The strongest are Opening and Coherence.

## 6. Independence
- **Level:** isolated subagents (Claude Code `book-genesis-blind-reader`, `book-genesis-auditor`, and general-purpose rubric evaluators), each in a fresh context with no targets, scores or verdicts in its packet.
- **Grade: B** (fresh contexts, one model family). No second model family was available. Isolation check: not needed (documented by the host).
- **Deviations, recorded honestly:**
  - Chapters were drafted in parallel, and the every-fifth-chapter panel reads were folded into the audit and the gate reads.
  - Gate reads had no previous-chapter tails.
  - Line-level prose revisions (ch0, ch2–7, ch9–16) were accepted without a separate A/B read.
  - The final polish was not re-measured.
  - The writers and the critics are the same model family as the orchestrator.

## 7. Disagreements and confidence
- The rubric evaluators agreed within 0.4 on every dimension (no 1.5-point disagreement).
- They disagreed with each other about whether the fiction-calibrated tells file should cap nonfiction prose. The second round found the tells under their limits.
- The hostile reader (Marcus) repeatedly asked for more credit to prior work. The book now credits:
  - algorithmic monoculture (Kleinberg & Raghavan);
  - correlated errors (Kim et al.);
  - Artificial Hivemind;
  - common-cause failure analysis and the beta factor;
  - intellectual debt (Zittrain);
  - Catanzaro's "algorithmic dead hands";
  - Tomašev et al. on agent economies;
  - armchair auditors and sousveillance;
  - regulatory budgets.
- Confidence: medium-high for Prose, Coherence, Opening and Voice; medium for Pacing, Emotion and Characters; low for Market (no verified comps).

## 8. Next move
- **Weakest dimension: Market (calibrated 6.3).** The single most useful intervention is to verify the comps and positioning against live sources (publisher pages and bestseller lists for *Nexus*, *Co-Intelligence*, *The Coming Wave*, *AI 2027* and 2025–26 titles), and to adopt the hook-test winner's framing (artifacts/13-positioning.md). Both are packaging decisions for the author, not prose fixes.
- **Second weakest: Pacing (6.7).** Break the identical chapter template in three or four chapters, for example by opening one chapter with the objection, or merging the evidence into the scene. Move more specification numbers into the appendix, which already exists for that purpose.
- Nothing here predicts sales. These numbers measure craft risk as judged by simulated readers from one model family; a human editor's read is the right next check.
