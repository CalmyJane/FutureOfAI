# Brief: THE GREAT INVERSION

## The idea (verbatim)
"My Idea is the Future of Humanity together with AI. It should be absolutely up-to-date and describe a future about 10 years from now and what AI has changed in the world and how people live then. … It should be innovative with new thoughts, new creative concepts that have good arguments. If some AI researcher reads the book he should be getting new ideas. Make up yourself what type of book it is … Should be quite long."
Author revision (2026-10-20): "No, too much novel, how about non-fiction? And the concepts also don't seem crazy, come up with something new that's worth writing a sachbuch about."

## Direction
- Working title: **The Great Inversion**
- Subtitle: *Eighteen Assumptions AI Will Overturn by 2036, and the Ideas We'll Need When It Does*
- Alternative titles: *Upside Down: The World in 2036*; *Inversions*
- Language: English
- Genre: ideas nonfiction (Sachbuch)
- Target: 85,000–100,000 words (planned 95,000)
- Structure: prologue + 18 chapters in five parts (Mind, Truth, Power, Matter and Work, Kinship) + epilogue; about 5,000 words per chapter
- Voice: first-person author, essayistic, evidence-led; each chapter opens with a 400–600-word 2036 vignette
- Each chapter contains: old assumption → 2026 evidence → inversion → named concept → strongest objection → "For researchers" box → what to do now

## Reader promise (one sentence)
Eighteen of humanity's oldest assumptions, from "evidence settles disputes" to "the dead stop acting", will flip by 2036; this book shows the evidence that it has started, what life looks like after, and the new ideas we will need.

## Panel
1. **Primary: Dana, 41, product manager in Toronto.** Reads big-idea nonfiction on purpose. Recent reads: *Nexus*, *Co-Intelligence*, *The Coming Wave*. Keeps reading for concepts she'll repeat at dinner. Quits on vague futurism and on chapters that restate the previous one.
2. **Hostile: Marcus, 52, ML researcher, skeptical of hype.** Recent reads: *AI 2027*, *If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies* (unconvinced). First objection: "Speculation dressed as analysis." Catches unsourced numbers, magical AGI, strawmen, and "concepts" that are just renamed old ideas.
3. **Adjacent: Leila, 34, reads behavioural economics and pop social science.** Recent reads: *Thinking, Fast and Slow*, *The Anxious Generation*. Keeps reading for surprising mechanisms and human stories. Quits on jargon.
4. **Casual (fixed).** Gives any book ten pages in an airport. Reads fast, skips description, has no patience for setup.
