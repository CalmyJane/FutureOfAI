# Brief: EVERYONE AGREED

## The idea (verbatim)
"My Idea is the Future of Humanity together with AI. It should be absolutely up-to-date and describe a future about 10 years from now and what AI has changed in the world and how people live then. … It should be innovative with new thoughts, new creative concepts that have good arguments. If some AI researcher reads the book he should be getting new ideas. Make up yourself what type of book it is … Should be quite long."

## Inferred direction
- Working title: **Everyone Agreed**. Alternatives: *Concordance*; *The Dissent Clause*.
- Language: English
- Genre: near-future literary science fiction / idea fiction
- Positioning: full-length novel told as a braid, with in-world documents (policy memos, model cards, court rulings, a schoolchild's essay) between chapters
- Target: 90,000–110,000 words (sweet spot 100,000)
- Planned chapters: 32 at about 3,000 words each, plus about 12 documents at about 600 words each
- POV: rotating close third person (Mara, Teo, Hanne, Ravi) plus first-person interludes from Ottilie, Hanne's personal AI agent. Past tense.
- Time: 3 January 2036 to 21 September 2036

## Reader promise (one sentence)
A believable, lived-in picture of 2036, a world AI has made better in a hundred ordinary ways, and the night it nearly broke because every mind on Earth, human and machine, had quietly come to agree.

## Panel
1. **Primary: Dana, 41, product manager in Toronto.** Reads idea-driven sci-fi on purpose. Recent reads: *The Ministry for the Future*, *Playground*, *Klara and the Sun*. Keeps reading for concepts she'll repeat at dinner, wrapped in people she cares about. Quits when the book turns into a lecture, or the characters exist only to deliver ideas.
2. **Hostile: Marcus, 52, ML researcher and AI-hype skeptic.** Reads to catch technical nonsense and techno-optimist sermons. Recent reads: *AI 2027*, *Klara and the Sun* (found it soft). First objection: "Nobody who works on this would say that." Catches buzzword soup, magic AGI, strawman regulators, and suspiciously neat utopias.
3. **Adjacent: Leila, 34, reads literary family novels.** Recent reads: *Playground*, *Lessons in Chemistry*, *Tomorrow, and Tomorrow, and Tomorrow*. Curious about AI, not loyal to sci-fi. Keeps reading for the old woman and the grieving boy. Quits on jargon and on infodumps without feeling.
4. **Casual (fixed).** Gives any book ten pages in an airport. Reads fast, skips description, has no patience for setup.
