## Comps (provisional hypotheses, from recall)
1. *The Ministry for the Future*, Kim Stanley Robinson, 2020, Orbit: braided chapters with in-world documents, an argument-rich near future. Our structural model. Too old for a strict comp, so pair it with a recent title.
2. *Playground*, Richard Powers, 2024: literary novel where AI is a lived presence. Comp for the emotional register.
3. *Klara and the Sun*, Kazuo Ishiguro, 2021: AI interiority and care. Comp for the Ottilie interludes. Probably too big to use as a query comp.
4. *AI 2027*, Kokotajlo et al., 2025: adjacent nonfiction scenario. Shows the audience for concrete near-term AI futures.

## Positioning
- **Gap:** recent AI fiction is either intimate (Ishiguro, Powers) or catastrophic (scenario fiction). No novel we know of shows a mostly good AI decade in granular daily detail AND locates its danger in something new and testable: correlated agreement, not rogue intent.
- **Target reader:** adults who read idea fiction and people who work in tech or policy.
- **Reader job:** fascination (primary) and empathy (secondary).
- **Length:** 100,000 words fits the SF row. *Ministry* runs longer, which is an unverified hypothesis.
- **Risks:** a crowded "AI novel" moment, and fast-dating detail. We hedge the second by grounding the book in mechanisms rather than brand names. Both are unverified hypotheses.
