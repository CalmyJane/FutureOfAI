# Positioning and hook test

## Hook test (simulated signal, not a market measurement)
Four packages were read blind by four simulated readers (primary, hostile, adjacent, casual; one model family; grade B), each together with the shared opening of about 250 words. Full detail: evaluations/panel-hook-test.md.

| Package | Yes answers (click/buy/keep reading, out of 12) | Favourite of |
|---|---|---|
| **2. Two Hundred Million Acts of Loyalty** | **12** | all four |
| 1. When Everything Goes Right | 10 | none |
| 4. Everyone Agreed | 6 | none |
| 3. The Long Minute | 4 | none |

**Winner:** Package 2. **Runner-up:** Package 1.

**Why it won:** it was the only package that puts a person and a checkable number in the reader's hand before any argument ("Hanne's assistant saved her four cents. So did everyone else's."; "thirteen times what the grid was built to survive"), and the opening pays off the blurb immediately. The hostile reader valued its honesty that some fixes are "borrowed from power engineers and pilots". The adjacent reader called it "small-choice, big-effect", the behavioural-economics hook she buys books for.

**Fixes the readers asked for, now applied in artifacts/12:**
- explain the figures: two hundred million *decisions* across the night, from ninety million *devices*;
- "Perfectly Obedient" became "Obedient";
- Package 1's "open problems for researchers" line folded into the Package 2 blurb;
- Package 1's logline kept as a secondary line.

## Recommendation
Adopt **Two Hundred Million Acts of Loyalty** as the title (chapter 1 already carries that name), with *When Everything Goes Right* as the series line or the prologue's thesis. This is the author's decision, and the manuscript still uses the working title until the author agrees.

## Comps and category
- **Category:** Technology & Society / Current Affairs; secondary Popular Economics and Science Policy.
- **Comps (provisional hypotheses; NOT yet verified against current sales lists):**
  - *Nexus* (Harari, 2024): ambition;
  - *Co-Intelligence* (Mollick, 2024): clarity and accessibility. Among the most-shelved AI books on Goodreads (checked 2026-10-20);
  - *The Coming Wave* (Suleyman, 2023): policy reader; also among the most-shelved on Goodreads;
  - *AI 2027* (Kokotajlo et al., 2025): concrete near-term scenarios.
  
  Per the comp rules, at least one recent title (2025–26) with verifiable traction should be added after a live check.
- **Subtitle convention:** the subtitle states the promise ("Why Obedient AI Can Still Fail Us All, and What to Build Before 2036"); this is a convention, not a statistic.
- **Title check:** "Two Hundred Million Acts of Loyalty" can be said aloud and typed from memory after one hearing.

## Risks
- A crowded AI shelf.
- Fast-dating 2026 facts: mitigated by keeping dated evidence in "Where we are in 2026" sections and Notes.
- A dual audience (general readers and researchers): mitigated by the appendix and the clearly marked researcher boxes.

All of these are unverified hypotheses. The market gate remains **unverified** because the comps were not checked against live lists.
