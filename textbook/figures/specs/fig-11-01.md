# fig-11-01

- **type:** diagram
- **purpose:** Show students the three families of AI risk used by the International AI Safety Report, with concrete examples in each, and where each is treated in the book.
- **caption:** Figure 11.1: The three families of AI risk used by the International AI Safety Report, with examples of each and the sections of this book that discuss them. The 2026 edition narrowed its focus to emerging risks of general-purpose AI and no longer covers bias as a separate topic. Source: [@bengio2025international; @bengio2026international]
- **content:**
  - Layout: top-to-bottom tree. One root box at top, three column boxes beneath, each with a stack of example boxes below it.
  - Root box: "AI risks"
  - Three arrows from root down to three category boxes (left → right):
    1. "Malicious use"
    2. "Malfunctions"
    3. "Systemic risks"
  - Under "Malicious use" (stacked boxes, each with a small grey section tag at right):
    - "Fraud and deepfakes" — tag "Ch. 9, 11.4"
    - "Manipulation" — tag "Ch. 9"
    - "Cyber operations" — tag "11.4"
    - "Bio/chem weapons" — tag "11.4"
  - Under "Malfunctions":
    - "Unreliable outputs" — tag "11.2"
    - "Bias" (draw with dashed border; footnote marker *) — tag "11.3"
    - "Loss of control" — tag "11.6"
  - Under "Systemic risks":
    - "Labour market" — tag "Ch. 8"
    - "Monoculture, correlated errors" — tag "11.5"
    - "Over-reliance (automation bias)" — tag "11.5"
    - "Market concentration" — tag "Ch. 7"
  - Small note at bottom-left: "* in 2025 report; outside 2026 report's scope"
  - Optional thin horizontal band at the bottom spanning all three columns, labelled "Categories interact (11.7)".
  - Category names and subcategories: 2025 report sections 2.1 (malicious use: fake content, manipulation, cyber offence, biological and chemical attacks), 2.2 (malfunctions: reliability, bias, loss of control), 2.3 (systemic: labour market, R&D divide, market concentration and single points of failure, environment, privacy, copyright); 2026 report sections 2.1–2.3 (bias omitted, scope note). "Monoculture, correlated errors" and "over-reliance" are placed under systemic risks following 2025 §2.3.3 ("single points of failure") and 2026 §2.3.2 ("risks to human autonomy", which covers automation bias).
