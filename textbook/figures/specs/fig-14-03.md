# fig-14-03

- **type:** diagram
- **purpose:** Contrast conventional single-model evaluation with the proposed population-level evaluation (Proposal 14.1), so readers see that the second measures convergence of many copies on one action.
- **caption:** Figure 14.3: Two ways to evaluate AI agents. Left: conventional evaluation runs one model instance through many different tasks and reports average accuracy. Right: the proposed population-level evaluation runs many instances, each with a slightly different context, through one stress scenario and reports how concentrated their actions are. Diagram by the author.
- **content:**
  - Two panels side by side, titled "A. Conventional evaluation" (left) and "B. Population-level evaluation" (right).
  - Panel A: one box "One model instance" at left; arrows fanning out to a column of 5 small boxes "Task 1", "Task 2", "Task 3", "…", "Task N"; arrows converge into an output box "Average accuracy".
  - Panel B: a column of three small robot/agent icons with vertical dots between the second and the last, labelled "Copy 1 (user A)", "Copy 2 (user B)", "…", "Copy N (user Z)"; all arrows point to one central box "One stress scenario" (subtitle: "e.g. sudden price change", matching the chapter text); from it arrows lead to a small bar chart icon with three bars of unequal height labelled "Action X", "Action Y", "Action Z" (one bar much taller); output box "Action concentration = share choosing the most common action".
  - Layout: left to right within each panel; panels separated by a thin vertical rule.
  - Short labels only, greyscale.
