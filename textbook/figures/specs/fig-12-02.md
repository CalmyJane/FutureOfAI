# fig-12-02

- **type:** diagram
- **purpose:** Give an intuitive picture of how a sparse autoencoder (SAE) turns overlapping (superposed) neuron activations into a much larger set of mostly-inactive, interpretable features, which researchers then label.
- **caption:** Figure 12.2: How a sparse autoencoder looks for features. Activations from one layer of a language model, in which several concepts overlap, are expanded into a much larger set of candidate features, of which only a few are active for any given input, and then reconstructed. Researchers then label features by inspecting the inputs that activate them. Schematic, not to scale. Diagram by the author, based on [@bricken2023towards] and [@templeton2024scaling].
- **content:**
  - Layout: left to right, four stages.
  - Stage 1 (far left): a text snippet box labelled "Input text" containing the example phrase: "Driving over the Golden Gate Bridge".
  - Arrow to Stage 2: a short vertical column of 6 circles labelled "Model neurons (one layer)". Shade circles in mixed grey levels to show that many are partly active. Small annotation beside: "Concepts overlap (superposition)".
  - Arrow labelled "Encoder" to Stage 3: a much taller column of ~20 small squares labelled "Candidate features (many more)". Only 3 squares are filled dark; the rest are white/empty. Annotation: "Sparse: few active".
    - Next to the three dark squares, place short tag labels: "Golden Gate Bridge", "Driving / cars", "San Francisco".
  - Arrow labelled "Decoder" to Stage 4: a column of 6 circles identical in layout to Stage 2, labelled "Reconstructed activations".
  - Under Stage 3, a bracket with the note: "Labelled by inspecting top-activating examples".
  - Under the arrow between Stage 2 and Stage 4, a thin dashed line labelled "Reconstruction error = unexplained part".
  - Greyscale; feature labels in small italic.
  - Note for illustrator: the three example feature labels are illustrative (the Golden Gate Bridge feature is documented in [@templeton2024scaling]; the other two are illustrative and the diagram is labelled schematic).
