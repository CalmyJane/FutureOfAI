# fig-12-01

- **type:** diagram
- **purpose:** Show students that AI safety relies on several imperfect, overlapping safeguards ("defence in depth"), and that harm gets through only when the weaknesses in every layer line up. It also works as a map of the chapter's sections.
- **caption:** Figure 12.1: The "Swiss cheese" model of layered AI safety. Each slice is an imperfect safeguard with holes; harm occurs only when the holes in every layer line up. The layers shown are those discussed in this chapter. Source: adapted from [@reason2000human] and [@bengio2026international].
- **content:**
  - Layout: left to right. Five or six vertical slices (drawn as slightly tilted rounded rectangles, like slices of cheese) spaced evenly across the width, each with 2–4 irregular holes of different sizes.
  - Far left: a small box or arrow-start labelled "Hazard" (or "Risky behaviour").
  - Slices, left to right, with labels underneath each (≤ 4 words) and a small section tag in grey:
    1. "Alignment training" (12.1: RLHF, constitutions)
    2. "Pre-release evaluations" (12.5: red-teaming, capability tests)
    3. "Interpretability audits" (12.3)
    4. "Reasoning monitoring" (12.4: chain-of-thought monitors)
    5. "Deployment safeguards" (filters, access controls)
    6. "Governance frameworks" (12.6: thresholds, external testing)
  - Arrow 1 (solid): starts at "Hazard", passes through a hole in slice 1, is blocked by slice 2 (ends with a small bar/stop mark). Label: "Stopped".
  - Arrow 2 (dashed): starts at "Hazard", passes through aligned holes in all six slices, ends at a box on the far right labelled "Harm".
  - Small note under the whole diagram: "No single layer is complete; layering reduces the chance that all fail together."
  - Greyscale only; holes as white ellipses on light-grey slices.
