# fig-12-01

- **type:** diagram
- **purpose:** Show readers that AI safety relies on several imperfect, overlapping safeguards ("defence in depth"), and that harm gets through only when the weaknesses in every layer line up. It also works as a map of the chapter's sections.
- **caption:** Figure 12.1: The "Swiss cheese" model of layered AI safety. Each slice is an imperfect safeguard with holes; harm occurs only when the holes in every layer line up. The layers, from alignment training to governance frameworks, correspond to Sections 12.1 and 12.3–12.7; interpretability, drawn dashed, is still a research tool. Layering protects only as far as the layers fail independently. Schematic by the author, adapted from [@reason2000human] and [@bengio2026international].
- **content:**
  - Layout: left to right. Five or six vertical slices (drawn as slightly tilted rounded rectangles, like slices of cheese) spaced evenly across the width, each with 2–4 irregular holes of different sizes.
  - Far left: a small box or arrow-start labelled "Hazard" (or "Risky behaviour").
  - Slices, left to right, with labels underneath each (≤ 4 words) and a small section tag in grey:
    1. "Alignment training" (12.1: RLHF, constitutions)
    2. "Pre-release evaluations" (12.3: red-teaming, capability tests)
    3. "Interpretability audits" (12.4: research stage) — drawn with a dashed outline and lighter fill
    4. "Reasoning monitoring" (12.5: chain-of-thought monitors)
    5. "Deployment safeguards" (12.6: control protocols, containment)
    6. "Governance frameworks" (12.7: thresholds, external testing)
  - Arrow 1 (solid): starts at "Hazard", passes through a hole in slice 1, is blocked by slice 2 (ends with a small bar/stop mark). Label: "Stopped".
  - Arrow 2 (dashed): starts at "Hazard", passes through aligned holes in all six slices, ends at a box on the far right labelled "Harm".
  - Small two-line note under the whole diagram: "No single layer is complete; layering helps most when layers fail independently. / A model that recognises tests, or monitors from the same model family, can line up the holes (11.5, 12.3)."
  - Greyscale only; holes as white ellipses on light-grey slices.
