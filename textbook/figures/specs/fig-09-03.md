# fig-09-03

- **type:** two-panel horizontal bar chart (one panel per study, each with its own x-axis; error bars where available)
- **purpose:** Show readers the size of AI persuasion effects found in recent large experiments, that conversation beats a static message, that effects in a high-salience US presidential race were smaller than in Canada, Poland or a ballot measure, without inviting comparison across studies that measure different quantities.
- **caption:** Figure 9.3: Persuasive effects of AI in two sets of experiments, in points on 0–100 attitude or preference scales, with 95% confidence intervals where reported. Panel A shows treatment-minus-control effects on policy attitudes: conversation with AI moved attitudes more than a static AI-written message. Panel B shows the gap between participants assigned to AI advocates for opposing candidates or positions, which is roughly twice a one-sided effect; the gap was smallest in the high-salience US presidential race. The panels have separate axes and should not be compared with each other. Source: @hackenburg2025levers; @lin2025persuading.

## content

Panel A x-axis: "Effect: treatment minus control (points, 0–100 scale)", linear, 0 to 20. Panel B x-axis: "Gap between pro-A and pro-B AI advocates (points, 0–100 scale)", linear, 0 to 32. y-axis: one bar per row within each panel.

**Group A — Hackenburg et al. (2025), UK, 707 policy issues; effect = treatment minus no-conversation control** [hackenburg2025levers, accepted manuscript, LSE Research Online]

| Bar label | Value (points) | 95% CI | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Static AI message (GPT-4o) | 6.1 | not plotted | static-message effect in study 1 |
| Static AI message (GPT-4.5) | 6.9 | not plotted | static-message effect in study 3 |
| Average AI conversation | 9.4 | not plotted | average across conditions tested |
| "Maximal persuasion" AI conversation | 15.9 | not plotted | estimated, cross-fit ML; 26.1 among initial disagreers |

**Group B — Lin et al. (2025), election experiments; effect = difference between pro-candidate-A and pro-candidate-B AI conditions (or vs control)** [lin2025persuading]

| Bar label | Value (points) | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| US 2024 presidential (Harris vs Trump), n = 2,306 | 2.88 | 2.02–3.74 |
| Canada 2025 (Carney vs Poilievre), baseline | 10.05 | 7.52–12.57 |
| Poland 2025 (Trzaskowski vs Nawrocki), baseline | 9.94 | 6.68–13.20 |
| Massachusetts psychedelics ballot measure, n = 193 non-extreme voters (pro vs anti AI) | 22.73 | 17.00–28.46 |

**Reference line:** none. (An earlier draft drew Kalla & Broockman's near-zero field-experiment estimate at 0; it was dropped because it measures vote choice in field contact, not attitudes in survey experiments, and coincided with the axis.)

**Footnote (text, no bars):** "Durability: 36–42% of the conversation effect remained after one month (A); slightly more than one third after more than a month (B, US). Whiskers: 95% CIs."

Style: greyscale; Group A bars light grey, Group B bars dark grey; CIs as thin black whiskers.
