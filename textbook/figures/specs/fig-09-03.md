# fig-09-03

- **type:** two-panel horizontal bar chart (one panel per study, each with its own x-axis; error bars where available)
- **purpose:** Show readers the size of AI persuasion effects found in recent large experiments, that conversation beats a static message, that effects in a high-salience US presidential race were smaller than in Canada, Poland or a ballot measure, without inviting comparison across studies that measure different quantities.
- **caption:** Figure 9.3: Persuasive effects of AI in two sets of experiments, in points on 0–100 attitude or preference scales, with 95% confidence intervals where reported. Panel A shows treatment-minus-control effects on policy attitudes: in the same experiment, conversation with GPT-4o moved attitudes more than a static message written by it; the third bar averages all conditions tested across 19 models, and the hatched bar is the authors' model-based estimate for a combination of the most effective levers, not an observed arm. Panel B shows the gap between participants assigned to AI advocates for opposing candidates or positions, which is roughly twice a one-sided effect; the gap was smallest in the high-salience US presidential race. The panels have separate axes and should not be compared with each other. Source: @hackenburg2025levers; @lin2025persuading.

## content

Panel A x-axis: "Effect: treatment minus control (points, 0–100 scale)", linear, 0 to 20. Panel B x-axis: "Gap between pro-A and pro-B AI advocates (points, 0–100 scale)", linear, 0 to 32. y-axis: one bar per row within each panel.

**Group A — Hackenburg et al. (2025), UK, 707 policy issues; effect = treatment minus no-conversation control** [hackenburg2025levers, accepted manuscript, LSE Research Online]

| Bar label | Value (points) | 95% CI | Note |
|---|---|---|---|

| Conversation, average of all conditions (19 models) | 9.4 | not reported | "the 9.4pp average condition we tested" |
