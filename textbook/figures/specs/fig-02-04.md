# Figure 2.4: Training versus validation error (overfitting)

- **type:** line chart (schematic, conceptual)
- **purpose:** Show the classic signature of overfitting: training error keeps falling while validation error falls, bottoms out and rises; early stopping chooses the validation minimum.
- **caption:** Figure 2.4: The classic overfitting curve. Training error (solid line) keeps falling as training continues, while validation error (dashed line) falls, reaches a minimum and then rises as the network starts to memorise quirks of the training data. Early stopping halts training near the validation minimum. Schematic, not to scale. Diagram by the author.
- **content:**
  - x-axis: "Training time (epochs)", no numeric ticks. y-axis: "Error", no numeric ticks. Linear schematic axes.
  - Solid line "Training error": starts high on the left, decreases steeply then more slowly, approaching near zero on the right; monotonically decreasing.
  - Dashed line "Validation error": starts at about the same height, decreases alongside the training line but stays above it, reaches a minimum at roughly 40% of the x-axis, then rises gently to the right.
  - Vertical dotted line at the validation minimum, labelled "early stopping point".
  - Region left of the dotted line lightly shaded and labelled "underfitting → good fit"; region right labelled "overfitting".
  - Bracket between the two lines at the right end labelled "generalisation gap".
  - No data values: this is a conceptual illustration (pattern described in Goodfellow et al. 2016, sec. 7.8, but no data taken from it).
