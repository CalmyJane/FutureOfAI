# fig-14-01

- **type:** bar chart (grouped, horizontal)
- **purpose:** Show students that the framing of a survey question moved AI researchers' aggregate forecasts far more than one year of new evidence did.
- **caption:** Figure 14.1: Aggregate year by which AI researchers gave a 50% chance to two milestones, in surveys run in 2022 and 2023. "High-level machine intelligence" asks when machines will do every task better and more cheaply than humans; "full automation of labour" asks when every occupation will be automatable. The gap between framings is larger than the change between years. Source: [@grace2025thousands].
- **content:**
  - Two groups (rows), each with two bars (2022 survey, 2023 survey).
  - Group 1, label "High-level machine intelligence (HLMI)": 2022 survey = 2060; 2023 survey = 2047. (The 2023 survey reports 2047, "13 years earlier" than the 2022 estimate; 2060 is therefore 2047 + 13.) Source: grace2025thousands.
  - Group 2, label "Full automation of labour (FAOL)": 2022 survey = 2164; 2023 survey = 2116. Source: grace2025thousands.
  - Axis: "Year at which aggregate forecast reaches 50% probability", linear scale, range 2020 to 2180, ticks every 20 years. Optional vertical reference line at 2026 labelled "today".
  - Legend: "2022 survey (738 respondents)", "2023 survey (2,778 respondents)".
  - Value labels at the end of each bar.
  - Greyscale: 2022 bars light grey, 2023 bars dark grey.
