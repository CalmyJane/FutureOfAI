# fig-14-01

- **type:** dot plot (two rows; a bar starting at an arbitrary year would give bar length no meaning)
- **purpose:** Show how AI researchers' aggregate forecasts for two framings of "human-level AI" have moved across three survey rounds, and how far apart the framings remain.
- **caption:** Figure 14.1: Aggregate year by which AI researchers gave a 50% chance to two milestones, in surveys run in 2022, 2023 and 2024 (the 2024 round, fielded in December 2024, had 1,580 respondents). "High-level machine intelligence" asks when machines will do every task better and more cheaply than humans; "full automation of labour" asks when every occupation will be automatable. Both forecasts moved earlier in each round, and the gap between the two framings narrowed to 56 years in 2024. Source: [@grace2025thousands; @grace2026advanced].
- **content:**
  - Two rows, each with three dots (2022: open circle; 2023: grey circle; 2024: black circle) joined by a grey line.
  - HLMI: 2022 = 2060, 2023 = 2047 (grace2025thousands, Table 6); 2024 = 2042 (grace2026advanced, Section 3.3: "a 50% chance in 2042").
  - FAOL: 2022 = 2164, 2023 = 2116 (grace2025thousands); 2024 = 2098 (grace2026advanced: "a 50% chance in 2098").
  - Labels: 2022 and 2023 values above their dots, 2024 below (2042 and 2047 are close).
  - Axis: "Year at which aggregate forecast reaches 50% probability", 2020–2180, ticks every 20 years; dashed reference line at 2026 labelled "today".
  - Legend: "2022 survey", "2023 survey", "2024 survey". Note: "Values as reported for each round; the 2024 report's recomputation moves earlier rounds by up to six years." (grace2026advanced recomputes 2022 HLMI as 2059, 2022 FAOL as 2158, 2023 FAOL as 2115.)
  - Respondent counts per question are no longer shown: the 2024 report gives the number of participants assigned each framing (HLMI 537 + 471; FAOL 233 + 234), which is not comparable with the 2022–23 counts of respondents answering.
