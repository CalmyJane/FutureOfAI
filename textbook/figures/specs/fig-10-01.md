# Figure 10.1 spec

- **type:** bar chart (two panels, side by side or stacked)
- **purpose:** Show students that AI tutoring can raise or lower measured learning depending on tool design and on what is measured (assisted practice vs unassisted exam), and give the scale of effects in two other trials.
- **caption:** Figure 10.1: Two kinds of evidence on AI tutors. Panel A: in a randomised trial in a Turkish high school, students using an unrestricted GPT-4 interface ("GPT Base") or a hint-giving version ("GPT Tutor") scored far higher than a control group during practice, but in the later unassisted exam the GPT Base group scored 17% lower than the control group and the GPT Tutor group about the same. Panel B: estimated effects of two other tutoring trials in standard deviations. The same underlying technology can raise or lower measured learning depending on design and on what is measured. Source: @bastani2025generative; @desimone2025chalkboards; @kestin2025ai.

## content

### Panel A: Turkey high-school mathematics RCT (Bastani et al. 2025, PNAS, Table 1)
Grouped bar chart. x-axis groups: "Assisted practice", "Unassisted exam". Two bars per group: "GPT Base", "GPT Tutor". y-axis: "Change vs control group (%)", linear scale, from -30 to +140, with a zero line drawn clearly.

| Phase | Arm | Value (% vs control) | Underlying regression coefficient (score out of 1) | Control mean | Significance | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Assisted practice | GPT Base | +48 | +0.137 | 0.284 | p < 0.01 | bastani2025generative |
| Assisted practice | GPT Tutor | +127 | +0.361 | 0.284 | p < 0.01 | bastani2025generative |
| Unassisted exam | GPT Base | -17 | -0.054 | 0.321 | p < 0.05 | bastani2025generative |
| Unassisted exam | GPT Tutor | -1 (computed: -0.004 / 0.321 = -1.2%; label "≈0, n.s.") | -0.004 | 0.321 | not significant | bastani2025generative |

Label bars with the values (+48%, +127%, −17%, ≈0 n.s.). Note under panel: "Nearly 1,000 students, grades 9–11, autumn 2023; classes assigned to arms, students randomly assigned to classes." (Corrected in fact-check 2, 23 Oct 2026: the paper assigned classes to arms by matching on observables; randomness comes from the school's random allocation of students to classes, PNAS footnote to the design section.)
