# fig-04-01

- **type:** line chart (schematic)
- **purpose:** Show readers what a scaling law looks like on log-log axes: a near-straight downward line, fitted on small runs and extrapolated to a large planned run, flattening towards an irreducible loss.
- **caption:** Figure 4.1: Schematic of a scaling law. On logarithmic axes, test loss falls along an approximately straight line as training compute grows. Small, cheap runs (filled dots) are used to fit the line, which is then extended to predict the loss of a planned large run (hollow dot). The curve flattens towards an irreducible loss that more compute cannot remove. Schematic by the author, not to scale, after the power-law fits of @kaplan2020scaling and the irreducible-loss form used by @henighan2020scaling and @openai2023gpt4.
- **content:**
  - SCHEMATIC, NOT TO SCALE. No real data; do not put numbers on the axes.
  - x-axis label: "Training compute (log scale)"; tick labels only as relative markers "×10" between gridlines (optional), no absolute values.
  - y-axis label: "Test loss (log scale)"; no numeric values.
  - Curve: a smooth curve of the form loss = a·C^(−b) + c. Across most of the x-range it appears as a straight line sloping down from upper left to lower right; at the far right it bends gently and approaches a horizontal dashed line.
  - Horizontal dashed line near the bottom, labelled "Irreducible loss".
  - 6–8 filled dots on the left third of the curve, labelled (one label for the group) "Small training runs".
  - Solid line segment through the dots labelled "Fitted trend"; continuing as a dotted line to the right labelled "Extrapolation".
  - One hollow dot at the far right on the dotted extension, labelled "Planned large run (prediction)".
  - Layout: single panel, landscape; labels ≤ 4 words; greyscale.
- **sources:** conceptual, based on the power-law form described in kaplan2020scaling and openai2023gpt4 (L(C) = aC^b + c with an irreducible term). No data values.
