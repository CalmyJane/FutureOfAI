# fig-08-01

- **type:** diagram
- **purpose:** Show that "exposure" is only the first link in a chain from technical potential to economic outcomes, and that causal evidence about AI becomes scarcer at each later step.
- **caption:** Figure 8.1: From exposure to economic outcomes. Exposure measures estimate what AI could do; adoption determines whether it is used; task-level productivity effects determine what happens when it is used; and firm decisions translate those effects into employment, wages and output. Data on firms and the economy are plentiful, but causal evidence linking them to AI becomes scarcer at each step. Diagram by the author.

## content

Layout: left → right chain of five boxes connected by single arrows. Beneath the chain, a horizontal wedge/bar spanning the full width that narrows from left to right, labelled "Evidence on AI's own effects" (data are plentiful at every step; what thins out is evidence attributing outcomes to AI, as the caption says) (wide at left, thin at right).

Boxes (label, then a small grey sub-label underneath):
1. **Exposure** — sub-label: "What AI could do"
2. **Adoption** — sub-label: "Is it used?"
3. **Task productivity** — sub-label: "Time, quality, output"
4. **Firm response** — sub-label: "Hire, reassign, expand"
5. **Economy-wide outcomes** — sub-label: "Jobs, wages, GDP"

Arrows: 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 (solid).

Small example labels in italics under each box (≤ 4 words each):
1. *Exposure indices*
2. *Surveys, usage logs*
3. *Experiments (RCTs)*
4. *Firm data*
5. *Payroll, national accounts*

Above boxes 4 and 5, a dashed bracket labelled "Substitute or complement?" to indicate that the same task-level gain can lead to fewer or more jobs.

Greyscale, no colour coding required.

_Revision after external review (Oct 2026): caption and wedge label now say that causal evidence linking outcomes to AI, not data as such, thins out along the chain (review B3)._
