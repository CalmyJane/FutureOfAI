# Figure 3.1 — An embedding-space sketch

- **type:** diagram (2-D scatter sketch, schematic)
- **purpose:** Give an intuition that embeddings place words as points, that similar words cluster, and that some relationships appear as consistent directions (King − Man + Woman ≈ Queen).
- **caption:** Figure 3.1: An embedding space, sketched in two dimensions. Words used in similar ways sit close together, and some relationships (such as male to female, or country to capital) appear as roughly parallel arrows. Real embeddings have hundreds or thousands of dimensions. Schematic, not to scale. Source: illustrating findings of [@mikolov2013linguistic; @mikolov2013efficient]
- **content:**
  - Unlabelled axes (no numbers); small note in corner: "2-D sketch of a space with hundreds of dimensions".
  - Cluster 1 (upper left), points labelled: "king", "queen", "man", "woman". Draw arrow man→woman and a parallel arrow king→queen (same length, same direction, dashed). Label the arrows "male → female".
  - Cluster 2 (right): "France", "Paris", "Germany", "Berlin", "Italy", "Rome". Draw parallel arrows France→Paris, Germany→Berlin, Italy→Rome, labelled "country → capital".
  - Cluster 3 (lower left): "cat", "dog", "horse" close together, labelled with a light ellipse "animals".
  - Optional small inset box: "king − man + woman ≈ queen".
  - Layout: three loose clusters, ample white space between them; greyscale; points as small filled circles, labels ≤ 1 word.

- **Correction 23 Oct 2026 (fact-check 2):** caption source now also cites @mikolov2013efficient, whose test set contains the country–capital relations drawn in Cluster 2 (Mikolov, Yih and Zweig 2013 report King − Man + Woman ≈ Queen but not country–capital). No drawing or data change.
