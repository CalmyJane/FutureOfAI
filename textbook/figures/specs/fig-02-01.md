# Figure 2.1: A single artificial neuron

- **type:** diagram
- **purpose:** Show students that a neuron is a small, followable calculation: inputs × weights, plus bias, through an activation function, giving one output. The worked numbers let them trace the arithmetic.
- **caption:** Figure 2.1: A single artificial neuron computing its output from two inputs. Each input is multiplied by its weight, the products and the bias are added (0.5 × 2 + (−1) × 1 + 0.2 = 0.2), and the activation function (here ReLU, which turns negative totals into zero) produces the output. With inputs 1 and 1 the total would be −0.3 and the output 0. Diagram by the author.
- **content:**
  - Layout: left → right.
  - Left: two circles stacked vertically, labelled "Input x₁ = 2" (top) and "Input x₂ = 1" (bottom).
  - Arrows from each input to a central large circle (the neuron). Label on top arrow: "weight 0.5"; on bottom arrow: "weight −1".
  - Small box above the central circle, arrow into it: "bias +0.2".
  - Inside the central circle, left half: "Σ  weighted sum". Below the circle, small annotation: "0.5×2 + (−1)×1 + 0.2 = 0.2".
  - Inside the central circle, right half (separated by a vertical line): a tiny ReLU graph (flat at zero for negative values, rising diagonal for positive), labelled "ReLU".
  - Arrow from the circle to the right ending at a box: "Output = 0.2".
  - Optional small inset at bottom right: "If inputs were 1 and 1: sum = −0.3 → output 0".
  - Greyscale; weights and bias in a slightly darker tone to show they are the adjustable numbers.
