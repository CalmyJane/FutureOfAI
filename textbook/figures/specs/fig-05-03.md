# fig-05-03

- **type:** line chart
- **purpose:** Show how quickly end-to-end success falls when every step of a multi-step task must succeed, even with high per-step reliability.
- **caption:** Figure 5.3: How per-step reliability compounds over a multi-step task. Each curve shows the probability that every step succeeds, for per-step success rates of 99, 95 and 90%, assuming steps fail independently and every step must succeed. Simple arithmetic, not measured data; real agents can recover from some errors, and their errors are rarely independent. Diagram by the author.
- **content:**
  - Data are computed, not measured: probability = p^n, where p is per-step success and n is the number of steps. No external source; the computation is arithmetic.
  - x-axis: "Number of steps in task", linear, 0 to 50, ticks every 10.
  - y-axis: "Probability whole task succeeds (%)", linear, 0 to 100, ticks every 20.
  - Three curves (distinguish by line style for greyscale: solid, dashed, dotted). Plot smooth curves through these exact points (values in %, rounded to one decimal):
    - p = 99% per step (solid): n=1: 99.0; n=5: 95.1; n=10: 90.4; n=20: 81.8; n=30: 74.0; n=40: 66.9; n=50: 60.5
    - p = 95% per step (dashed): n=1: 95.0; n=5: 77.4; n=10: 59.9; n=20: 35.8; n=30: 21.5; n=40: 12.9; n=50: 7.7
    - p = 90% per step (dotted): n=1: 90.0; n=5: 59.0; n=10: 34.9; n=20: 12.2; n=30: 4.2; n=40: 1.5; n=50: 0.5
  - Direct labels at the right end of each curve: "99% per step", "95% per step", "90% per step".
  - Optional marker: a small circle at (20, 35.8) on the 95% curve with label "20 steps at 95% ≈ 36%" (this example is used in the text).
  - Smoothness: intermediate points can be computed as p^n for every integer n from 1 to 50.
