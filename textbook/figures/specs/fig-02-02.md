# Figure 2.2: A small multilayer perceptron

- **type:** diagram
- **purpose:** Show how neurons are arranged in layers, that every connection carries its own weight, that each layer's weights form a matrix, and how parameters are counted.
- **caption:** Figure 2.2: A small multilayer perceptron with three inputs, one hidden layer of four neurons and two outputs. Each line is a connection with its own weight, and each hidden and output neuron also has a bias. The first layer's weights form a 4 × 3 matrix and the second layer's a 2 × 4 matrix, giving 3 × 4 + 4 × 2 = 20 weights and 4 + 2 = 6 biases: 26 parameters in all. Diagram and count by the author; network type as in @goodfellow2016deep [ch. 6].
- **content:**
  - Layout: left → right, three vertical columns of circles.
  - Column 1 (3 circles), header "Input layer"; circles labelled "x₁", "x₂", "x₃".
  - Column 2 (4 circles), header "Hidden layer"; each circle marked with a small "+b" to indicate a bias.
  - Column 3 (2 circles), header "Output layer"; each circle marked "+b"; labelled "Output 1", "Output 2".
  - Lines: every input circle connects to every hidden circle (12 lines); every hidden circle connects to every output circle (8 lines). Thin grey lines.
  - Highlight one line (input x₁ → top hidden neuron) in black with a label "one weight".
  - Small label above the connections between columns 1 and 2: "W₁: 4 × 3"; between columns 2 and 3: "W₂: 2 × 4" (the weight matrices of Section 2.2).
  - Bottom strip under the columns: "12 weights" (between columns 1 and 2), "8 weights" (between columns 2 and 3); "Total: 20 weights + 6 biases = 26 parameters".
  - Large arrow along the bottom pointing right: "forward pass".
