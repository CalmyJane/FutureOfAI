# fig-14-04

- **type:** diagram
- **purpose:** Explain the hypothetical mechanism behind Proposal 14.2: a random start delay spreads device starts when humans occasionally override it, but loyal agents reacting to the same price signal can override it simultaneously and create a load step larger than the grid's reference incident.
- **caption:** Figure 14.4: A hypothetical mechanism for correlated override (schematic, not to scale). Left: human owners cancel a random start delay at scattered moments, so device starts stay spread out. Right: agents acting loyally for each owner cancel the delay at the same price signal, so starts coincide and the combined load step can exceed the grid's reference incident. The delay rule shown is British; the continental European reference incident is used only to give a sense of scale. Diagram by the author, based on [@uk2021electric] and [@eu2017sogl].
- **content:**
  - Two panels, top/bottom or left/right: "A. Human overrides (today)" and "B. Agent overrides (hypothetical)".
  - Each panel: a horizontal time axis labelled "Time (minutes)", with a vertical dashed line labelled "Price signal".
  - Both panels: a grey bracket under the time axis marking the 10-minute default delay window, labelled "10 min".
  - Panel A: many small device-start ticks spread across the 10-minute window after the signal, plus three taller ticks with open circles, labelled "owner override", at scattered moments within the window. Below: a load sketch (axis "Load") in which load ramps from the starting level to the final level over the 10 minutes, labelled "Same total load arrives gradually over 10 min".
  - Panel B: a dense cluster of ticks immediately at the price-signal line, labelled "agents cancel delay together", plus a few remaining spread ticks. Below: the same final load reached almost at once, labelled "Load step".
  - Panel B: a vertical double-headed dimension arrow measured from the starting load (dotted guide), labelled "Reference incident: 3,000 MW (continental Europe, for scale)" (source: eu2017sogl, Commission Regulation (EU) 2017/1485, art. 153(2)(b)(i)); the step is taller than the arrow. The reference incident is a size of sudden change (instantaneous power deviation), not a load level, so it is drawn as a step height, not as a horizontal load threshold; the same devices start in both panels, so both reach the same final load.
  - Side note box: "UK rule: random delay up to 600 s by default; owner may cancel (SI 2021/1467, reg. 11)" (source: uk2021electric).
  - Footnote text in the figure: "Schematic, not to scale. Hypothetical mechanism, not a recorded event."
