# fig-14-04

- **type:** diagram
- **purpose:** Explain the hypothetical mechanism behind Proposal 14.2: a random start delay spreads device starts when humans occasionally override it, but loyal agents reacting to the same price signal can override it simultaneously and create a load step larger than the grid's reference incident.
- **caption:** Figure 14.4: A hypothetical mechanism for correlated override (schematic, not to scale). Left: human owners cancel a random start delay at scattered moments, so device starts stay spread out. Right: agents acting loyally for each owner cancel the delay at the same price signal, so starts coincide and the combined load step can exceed the grid's reference incident. Diagram by the author, based on [@uk2021electric] and [@doring2012dealing].
- **content:**
  - Two panels, top/bottom or left/right: "A. Human overrides (today)" and "B. Agent overrides (hypothetical)".
  - Each panel: a horizontal time axis labelled "Time (minutes)", with a vertical dashed line labelled "Price signal".
  - Panel A: many small device-start ticks spread across the axis (random delay up to 10 min after the signal), plus a few isolated ticks labelled "owner override" scattered at different times. Below the axis: a gentle ramp labelled "Load rises gradually".
  - Panel B: a dense cluster of ticks immediately at the price-signal line, labelled "agents cancel delay together". Below the axis: a sharp step labelled "Load step".
  - A horizontal reference line across the load sketch in both panels labelled "Reference incident: 3,000 MW" (source: doring2012dealing). In panel B the step crosses it; in panel A the ramp stays below it at any one moment.
  - Side note box: "Random delay up to 600 s by default, owner may cancel (UK SI 2021/1467, reg. 11)" (source: uk2021electric).
  - Footnote text in the figure: "Schematic, not to scale. Hypothetical mechanism, not a recorded event."
