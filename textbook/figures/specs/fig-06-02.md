# fig-06-02

- **type:** diagram
- **purpose:** Show the reader how AlphaFold 2 turns a single amino-acid sequence into a 3D structure: which inputs it uses, what the two internal representations are, where the Evoformer and the structure module sit, that the whole network is run several times (recycling), and that each output carries a confidence score. Supports the mechanism described in Section 6.2.
- **caption:** Figure 6.2: How AlphaFold 2 turns a sequence into a structure. The sequence is used to search databases for related sequences (the MSA) and templates; 48 Evoformer blocks exchange information between the MSA representation and the residue-pair representation; the structure module places each residue as a rigid body in 3D; the network's outputs are fed back in for further passes (recycling, four passes in all), and the final structure comes with a per-residue confidence score, pLDDT. Diagram by the author, after @jumper2021highly.
- **content:**
  - Layout: left→right pipeline in four columns, with one feedback arrow along the bottom.
  - Column 1 (inputs), stacked vertically:
    - Box "Input sequence" (small strip of letters, e.g. "MKTAYIAK…", purely illustrative).
    - Arrow from "Input sequence" to two database boxes: "Sequence databases" and "Structure database (PDB)".
    - Output of these: box "MSA (related sequences)" and box "Templates".
  - Column 2: two stacked rectangles:
    - "MSA representation" (drawn as a grid; label beneath: "rows = sequences, columns = residues").
    - "Pair representation" (drawn as a square grid; label beneath: "residue × residue").
    - Arrow from "MSA (related sequences)" into "MSA representation"; arrow from "Templates" into "Pair representation" (template pair features) and a thin arrow from "Templates" into "MSA representation" (template torsion angles are added as extra MSA rows); a thin arrow from "Input sequence" into both representations.
  - Column 3: a large box "Evoformer (48 blocks)" enclosing both representations' processing, with two-way arrows between an upper lane "MSA" and a lower lane "Pair". A small inset beside the pair lane: a triangle of three dots labelled i, j, k with edge ij highlighted and the note "triangle update: ik + jk → ij".
  - Column 4: box "Structure module" with label beneath "residues as rigid bodies; invariant point attention". Arrow out to an output box "3D structure + pLDDT" (draw a simple ribbon-like line; shade it in two tones with a small key "high / low confidence").
  - Feedback: arrow from the outputs of the Evoformer and the structure module (start it at the right edge of the "Structure module" box, not at the pLDDT output) back along the bottom to the input side of the two representations (column 2), labelled "Recycling (4 passes in all)". What is recycled is the pair representation, the first row of the MSA representation and the predicted backbone positions; the pLDDT score is not fed back.
  - Numbers on the diagram (all from jumper2021highly and its Supplementary Information): "48 blocks" on the Evoformer box; "4 passes in all" on the recycling arrow (N_cycle = 4, Suppl. Methods 1.10). No other numbers.
  - Short labels only (≤ 4 words where possible); greyscale; no logos.
- **source check:** MSA and templates as inputs; MSA representation (rows = sequences, columns = residues) and pair representation (N_res × N_res); 48 Evoformer blocks; triangle multiplicative update "uses two edges to update the missing third edge"; structure module with invariant point attention and residue gas (independent rotations and translations); recycling; per-residue pLDDT. All from Jumper et al. 2021 (Nature 596:583–589; PMC8371605). From the Supplementary Information (checked in fact-check 2, 23 Oct 2026): template torsion-angle features are concatenated to the MSA representation and template pair features added to the pair representation (Suppl. 1.4, Algorithm 2); recycling embeds the previous first MSA row, pair representation and Cβ positions (Algorithm 32); N_cycle = 4 (Suppl. 1.10); the 192-structure trajectory is 4 · 48 intermediate points from probe structure modules (Suppl. 1.14).
