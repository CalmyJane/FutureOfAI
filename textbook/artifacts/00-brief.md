# Brief: UNDERSTANDING AI (v4: textbook)

## Author's request (2026-10-22)
"Make it much less like a novel; the short portions should be purely scientific and well researched, with a clean source directory. Double-check everything in the book; make sure it could be used at a university to teach AI trends as a whole, still easy to read and with useful illustrations. There should also be some explanation of neural nets and LLMs, all well digestible. Don't get stuck on the current content and structure; rethink the whole thing."

## Decisions
- **Form:** a university course text (one semester, 14 chapters ≈ 14 weekly units). No fiction anywhere. Opening examples are real, documented cases.
- **Title:** *Understanding AI: Technology, Trends and the Decade Ahead*. Subtitle: *A Course Text*. Author: Jan Göbel.
- **Language:** English (British spelling). Reading level: first-year undergraduate, any discipline; no maths beyond school level. Optional "Going deeper" boxes for technical readers.
- **Length:** about 85,000–95,000 words.
- **Evidence standard:**
  - every non-obvious factual claim carries an author–date citation (APA 7);
  - one consolidated bibliography;
  - a separate fact-check pass verifies every citation against its source;
  - company claims, preprints and peer-reviewed findings are labelled as such;
  - the evidence is current to October 2026.
- **Pedagogy per chapter:**
  - learning objectives;
  - numbered sections;
  - 2–4 numbered figures that explain;
  - "Key concept", "Case study" and "Debate" boxes;
  - summary, key terms, discussion questions and annotated further reading.
- **Back matter:** a glossary and a bibliography.
- **From the previous version:** the original concepts (population-level evaluation, correlated failure and the M-1 criterion, competence reserves, signature capacity and others) survive only in the final chapter. There they are presented as clearly labelled research proposals, with their prior literature.
